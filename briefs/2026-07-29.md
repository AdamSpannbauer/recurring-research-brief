## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Raven: High-Recall Sequence Modeling with Sparse Memory Routing**  
   **Authors:** Arshia Afzal, Aviv Bick, Eric P. Xing, Volkan Cevher, Albert Gu  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Date:** July 28, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25357))  
   Raven targets the core weakness of linear-time sequence models: dense state updates smear information, while sliding-window attention preserves exact tokens only until eviction. The proposed model keeps a fixed set of memory slots and updates only learned, input-routed subsets, giving it a middle ground between SSM-style compression and attention-style explicit memory. The key result is not merely improved long-context benchmark scores, but preservation of high-recall behavior under extrapolation to contexts up to 16× training length. This looks like a clean architectural idea for memory-efficient foundation models.  
   **Why you should care:** Sparse, content-routed memory is becoming a serious alternative to “just extend attention,” and Raven gives a concrete design pattern.

2. **Bridging Compute- and Data-Optimal Pretraining**  
   **Authors:** Tian Qin, Kimia Hamidieh, David Alvarez-Melis  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Date:** July 28, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25271))  
   This paper reframes scaling laws for the data-constrained era. Instead of assuming endless fresh tokens, it introduces Compute-Data scaling laws with a token-effectiveness function measuring how useful derived tokens—via repetition or paraphrasing—are relative to fresh tokens. Fitting models from 14M to 600M parameters on Dolma-3, the authors show token effectiveness varies with model size, token/parameter ratio, and amount of derived data, and saturates as expansion grows. The result is a useful operational taxonomy: compute-bound, data-bound, and model-bound regimes.  
   **Why you should care:** It gives a sharper language for synthetic data, repeated epochs, and pretraining allocation than the usual Chinchilla-style compute-only story.

3. **Nonparametric Greedy Equivalence Search with Prior-Fitted Networks**  
   **Authors:** Mateusz Gajewski, Mateusz Olko  
   **Venue/source:** Causal Learning and Reasoning 2026, PMLR 323  
   **Date:** Proceedings released July 5, 2026  
   **Link:** ([proceedings.mlr.press](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v323/gajewski26a.html))  
   This is a strong bridge between tabular foundation models and causal discovery. The paper plugs TabPFN into nonparametric greedy equivalence search as a Bayesian likelihood estimator, exploiting PFNs’ posterior-predictive behavior rather than using them only as predictors. The authors report consistent gains over state-of-the-art nonparametric causal discovery methods across synthetic, simulated, and real datasets. Whether the empirical claims survive broader evaluation is open, but the idea is important: prior-fitted networks can act as fast amortized statistical subroutines inside classical causal search procedures.  
   **Why you should care:** It is one of the clearest examples of PFN/tabular-FM machinery becoming an inference engine, not just a classifier.

4. **Empirical Evaluation of Out-Of-Distribution Performance of Tabular Foundation Models**  
   **Authors:** Malena Loza, David Chushig-Muzo, Eva Milara, Luis Bote-Curiel, Luis Estrada-Petrocelli, Felipe Grijalva  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Date:** July 28, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26000))  
   This paper evaluates nine tabular foundation models—TabPFNv2/v2.5/v2.6/v3, TabICL/TabICLv2, Mitra, LimiX, and TabFM—under TableShift-style real-world distribution shifts. The headline finding is sobering: all models degrade systematically under label, socioeconomic, and geographic shifts; stronger IID performance remains predictive of OOD performance, but does not eliminate shift gaps. The study is narrower than BeyondArena, but timely because it includes the newest TFM variants and explicitly surfaces compute/memory deployment constraints.  
   **Why you should care:** It reinforces that the tabular-FM race is shifting from IID leaderboard wins to robustness, deployment feasibility, and shift-aware evaluation.

5. **Generator-Aligned Representation Interfaces for Diagnostic Soft Equivariance**  
   **Authors:** Weitao Li, Gong Cheng  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Date:** July 28, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25988))  
   GARI proposes a representation-level way to expose transformation generators to generic sequence backbones without building fully group-specific equivariant architectures. The paper formalizes a probe-specific soft-equivariance residual and instantiates GARI-Net with generator-indexed streams, shared processing, cross-stream exchange, and discrepancy-aware aggregation. Experiments span sequence reversal in genomics, image rotations/reflections, and 3D point-cloud transfer. The distinction between exact equivariance, robustness, and measurable representation consistency is the most useful contribution; it gives a diagnostic vocabulary for structured inductive bias in foundation backbones.  
   **Why you should care:** This is a potentially reusable interface pattern for injecting known symmetries into general architectures without hard-coding the whole model.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Tabular foundation models are entering the evaluation-hardening phase.** Recent work is less about beating XGBoost on IID splits and more about shift, memory/computation, privacy, causal discovery subroutines, time-series transfer, and query-centric utility.
- **Amortized models are becoming statistical primitives.** PFNs are now being used inside causal search and likelihood estimation, suggesting a broader pattern: foundation models as plug-in estimators for classical inference pipelines.
- **Data scarcity is becoming first-class in scaling-law work.** Derived tokens, paraphrases, repeated epochs, and synthetic pretraining are being analyzed as imperfect substitutes rather than assumed-good augmentation.
- **Representation diagnostics are moving from probes to operational interfaces.** GARI, SAE feature-effect geometry, Jacobian-lens work, and topology-based TabPFN diagnostics all point toward measuring what representations preserve under transformations, interventions, or context changes.
- **Agentic data systems are becoming benchmark-driven.** DataAgent-Bench, DBA-Bench, ABISS, Kalypso, AgentTrails, and recent cs.DB papers show a shift from text-to-SQL benchmarks to full data-workflow agents with provenance, ambiguity, access control, and serving constraints.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **HYSET: Tools Are Not Islands.** A set-level tool retriever for LLM agents that formulates tool selection as query-conditioned hyperedge prediction over co-invocation structure; worth tracking for data-analysis agents with large tool libraries. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25718))
- **Guiding Posterior Exploration with Optimizer-Derived Geometry.** Uses curvature information already accumulated by AdamW-style warmstarts to precondition Bayesian neural-network sampling, reducing or eliminating burn-in at negligible extra cost. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25312))
- **Data-Dependent Regret and Polyak Corrections for Constrained Online Convex Optimization.** TMLR-accepted theory paper giving tighter regret bounds and adaptive steps for per-round-feasible constrained OCO. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25480))
- **ERUnderstand.** A VLM benchmark for structured entity-relationship diagrams; useful as databases and multimodal document understanding collide. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/recent?show=1000))
- **AMPBench-MT.** Homology-controlled benchmark for antimicrobial peptide potency, spectrum, and safety prediction; a good example of benchmark design that controls sequence similarity rather than only reporting random-split accuracy. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent?show=2000&skip=0))
