## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **GEAR: Generative Expansion and Real Anchoring for Two-Stage Distillation of Tabular Foundation Models**  
   **Authors:** Qi Qin, Jiajie Zhu, Dali Chen, Yuzhao Zhang, Jia-Xing Han, Yu Su, Peng Zhang, Ying Yan, Yifan Sun  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv cs.LG/stat.ML  
   **Release date:** August 20, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record in today’s cs.LG listing.  
   **Summary:** This is a timely deployment-side answer to the “TFMs are accurate but expensive” problem. GEAR distills an in-context tabular foundation model into an ordinary MLP or tree model using a two-stage recipe: first query the teacher on generated covariate locations to expand coverage, then re-anchor the student using real labels and out-of-fold teacher predictions to avoid leakage. The risk certificate around generator volume versus fidelity is the key intellectual move: it treats synthetic expansion as a statistical design variable rather than just augmentation. Evaluations on TALENT and TabArena make it directly relevant to practical TFM adoption. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Why you should care:** If TFMs are to move from benchmark winners to production tools, this kind of teacher-query distillation may become the default compression interface.

2. **Sobolev Regularized Score Difference Estimation in Diffusion Models**  
   **Authors:** Chenghan Xie, Jose Blanchet, Renyuan Xu  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv stat.ML/cs.LG; accepted by ICML 2026  
   **Release date:** August 20, 2026 arXiv listing  
   **Link:** arXiv record in today’s stat.ML listing.  
   **Summary:** The paper targets a surprisingly central primitive: estimating differences between two score functions. Score differences appear in transfer learning, discriminator guidance, and post-training of diffusion models, but existing estimators can be inconsistent or unstable in small-sample/high-dimensional regimes. The authors introduce Sobolev regularization to stabilize score-difference learning, prove consistency and rates, and give a minimax lower bound. The empirical example—transfer for ECG signal generation—matters less than the abstraction: it supplies a statistically principled object for adapting pretrained generative models without relearning the whole score. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))  
   **Why you should care:** Score-difference estimation is becoming for diffusion post-training what influence functions are for supervised-model editing: a reusable adaptation primitive.

3. **Diffusion Models for High-Dimensional Clustered Data: Intrinsic-Dimension Adaptivity via Bayesian Classification**  
   **Authors:** Yuga Iguchi, Paul Fearnhead  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv stat.ML/cs.LG/math.ST  
   **Release date:** August 20, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record in today’s stat.ML listing.  
   **Summary:** This is a theory paper with a useful conceptual picture: denoising in a mixture distribution behaves like a dynamic Bayesian classifier that first mixes and then commits to a cluster once signal-to-noise crosses a threshold. Under Gaussian-mixture structure with cluster-specific low-dimensional covariance, the authors show KL error bounds scaling with the maximum intrinsic cluster dimension, up to logs, rather than ambient dimension. This connects empirical “diffusion finds the manifold” intuitions to a multi-cluster, heterogeneous-rank setting closer to real data than single-manifold analyses. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))  
   **Why you should care:** It sharpens when diffusion models should inherit low-dimensional structure—and when multimodality changes the story.

4. **Learned, Then Lost: A Measured Single-Example Counterfactual in Pre-training**  
   **Authors:** Zachary Speck, Asa Shepard  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv cs.LG  
   **Release date:** August 20, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record in today’s cs.LG listing.  
   **Summary:** Instead of estimating training-example influence, the authors run the expensive counterfactual: 32 GPT-2-scale pretraining runs differing by one injected row in one early batch. The model briefly learns the injected 194-token passage, but by the final checkpoint the measurable prediction advantage disappears, while weights still move substantially within the same basin. The result is small-scale but unusually clean: it separates transient memorization, final predictive behavior, and geometric displacement. For privacy, data attribution, and dataset valuation debates, this is a valuable reminder that “did this example affect weights?” and “can the model extract it later?” are different questions. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Why you should care:** It is one of the rare pretraining influence studies that measures the counterfactual directly rather than inferring it post hoc.

5. **Think Shallow, Solve Deep: Controlling Recurrent Dynamics for Reliable Test-Time Depth**  
   **Authors:** Ivan Viakhirev, Kirill Borodin, Amirah Almutairi, Serguei Barannikov, Maxim Abramov, Grach Mkrtchian  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv cs.LG/cs.CL; submitted to AAAI-27  
   **Release date:** August 20, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record in today’s cs.LG listing.  
   **Summary:** Recurrent-depth reasoning promises more computation at test time, but extra iterations can help, do nothing, or hurt. This paper makes that operational: estimate whether the learned recurrent operator is settling, marginal, or drifting; certify “depth safety” when per-step displacement is small relative to decoder margin; and train with a terminal fixed-point objective that nudges systems toward useful settling behavior. The empirical algorithmic-task results are early, but the diagnostic framing is strong: recurrent compute should be governed by measured dynamical regime, not just a loop count. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Why you should care:** It gives a concrete vocabulary for deciding when test-time depth is algorithmic computation versus uncontrolled recurrence.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Tabular foundation models are moving from “model” to “interface.”** GEAR, recent TabPFN localization/compression work, and relational-TFM activity all treat TFMs as teachers, priors, or queryable oracles rather than necessarily as deployed predictors.

- **Evaluation is becoming constructive and regenerable.** ClosureBench, CausalProfiler, Vericoding, CausalDS, and DataAgent-style benchmarks all push toward programmatically generated or formally checked ground truth, reducing static-test contamination.

- **Representation learning theory is increasingly about readout geometry and invariance validity.** Recent work on benchmark subspaces, concept dimension, multimodal contrastive expressivity, recurrent dynamics, and intrinsic-dimensional diffusion all ask: what is actually identifiable or usable from the learned representation?

- **Data-management ideas are flowing into ML-agent safety.** Agentic transactions, semantic isolation, proof-gated publication, SQL rewrite certification, and VLDB’s Dataflow Test-of-Time signal a convergence around correctness guarantees for systems that mix LLM inference with stateful data operations.

- **Generative-model theory is getting more local.** New diffusion analyses focus less on universal score matching and more on score differences, cluster commitment, intrinsic dimension, tail events, and transfer/post-training primitives.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **ClosureBench: A Constructive Benchmark for Compositional Graph Reasoning.** Programmatically generated graph-relational tasks with executable ground truth; useful for testing whether LLMs reason over structured relations or merely parse shallow graph descriptions. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **CausalProfiler.** Although not a brand-new artifact, the current ICML-accepted update adds an experiment and deserves tracking as a synthetic benchmark generator spanning observational, interventional, and counterfactual causal queries. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Lévy Attention.** A continuous-time attention operator that emits uncertainty in one pass for irregularly sampled time series; worth watching for medical/event-stream settings where MC dropout is too expensive. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **APICURON.** A reactive credit-attribution infrastructure for distributed biological data ecosystems; adjacent but relevant as a model for provenance and contribution accounting in curated ML datasets. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))
