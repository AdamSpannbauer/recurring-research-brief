## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **LoGIC: Budgeted Context Construction for Node-Level Graph In-Context Learning with Tabular Foundation Models**  
   **Authors:** Mingqi Yang, Zidong Guo, Jihui Yang, Wenming Zuo  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv cs.LG  
   **Release date:** September 9, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Summary:** LoGIC attacks a very practical bottleneck in graph/tabular foundation modeling: systems such as G2T-FM and GraphPFN encode nodes as tabular rows and rely on in-context learning, but full labeled contexts scale badly in memory and attention cost. The paper reframes node-level graph ICL as a budgeted context-construction problem, allocating labeled evidence and an unlabeled “halo” for local message passing. It uses structural, feature, and coverage retrieval channels, shares contexts across graph-local query clusters, and selects channel/budget without test labels. The main claim is that locally runnable, budgeted contexts can preserve full-context performance and enable frozen graph ICL on million-node graphs.  
   **Why you should care:** This is directly relevant to making tabular/relational foundation models deployable beyond toy-context regimes.

2. **When Retain Constraints Conflict: Mitigating Forget-Retain Interference in Tabular Data**  
   **Authors:** Zijie Liu, Jinhao Duan, Bingqi Shang, Xinming An, Sijia Liu, Tianlong Chen  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv cs.LG; EMNLP 2026 Findings  
   **Release date:** September 9, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Summary:** This paper identifies a tabular-specific unlearning failure mode: serialized records share schema slots, value ranges, and output spaces, so forget examples and nearby retain examples can depend on the same high-signal features. Standard retain-preservation constraints then actively oppose forgetting. The proposed Conflict-Aware Unlearning method relaxes preservation constraints on retained rows most in conflict with the forget set, and is evaluated for sample-level and feature-level unlearning on clinical and nonmedical tabular tasks. The paper is valuable because it treats tabular LLM unlearning not as generic text unlearning, but as schema-induced interference.  
   **Why you should care:** Privacy, deletion, and compliance stories for tabular foundation models need schema-aware retain-set design, not just stronger forgetting losses.

3. **Representation Learning for Sample-Efficient CATE Estimation by Leveraging Multiple Outcomes**  
   **Authors:** Maitreyi Swaroop, Shikha Bhat, Samantha Rodriguez, Tamar Krishnamurti, Bryan Wilder  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv cs.LG  
   **Release date:** September 9, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Summary:** The paper proposes using large historical multi-outcome datasets to learn lower-dimensional covariate representations before estimating conditional average treatment effects from small experiments. The key theoretical move is a surrogacy-style condition: if auxiliary outcomes preserve the covariate information relevant to treatment heterogeneity, replacing high-dimensional covariates with the learned representation identifies the original CATE. The authors also analyze the misspecified case as a bias–variance tradeoff: compression can still help when variance reduction dominates representation bias. Empirical work uses synthetic and semisynthetic medical data.  
   **Why you should care:** It gives a clean bridge between causal representation learning and the common applied setting of many historical outcomes but few experimental treatment labels.

4. **Newton Matching for Generative Modeling: A Unified Framework for Fine-Tuning and Sampling**  
   **Authors:** Zeyang Li, Yunan Wang, Paolo Giaretta, Navid Azizan  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv cs.LG  
   **Release date:** September 9, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Summary:** Newton Matching tries to unify reward-guided fine-tuning and sampling for generative models by viewing target distributions of the form \(\pi \propto \mu e^{\tau r}\) through a geometric optimization lens. Canonical velocities are treated as a manifold corresponding to terminal densities; under the transported Fisher–Rao metric, the reverse-KL Hessian becomes the metric, making Newton steps coincide with negative Fisher–Rao gradients. The paper derives finite-step density characterizations, global descent, local quadratic convergence, and sample-wise losses that avoid importance sampling or full-trajectory backprop.  
   **Why you should care:** This is a serious attempt to put RL-style generative-model alignment, sampling, and flow/diffusion matching into one reusable mathematical language.

5. **Concord: A Video Relational Algebra for Cross-Modal Query Optimization**  
   **Authors:** Sultan Muratbek, Charisse Ivana Yeung, Chanwut Kittivorawong, Alvin Cheung  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv cs.DB  
   **Release date:** September 9, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))  
   **Summary:** Concord defines a nested Video Relational Algebra over videos, transcripts, frames, and object tracks, then uses algebraic rewrites to avoid expensive multimodal-model calls. For narrated video, it can route through transcripts or use transcripts to identify clips; for cross-camera queries, it replaces whole-video MLLM joins with detection/tracking plus relational joins. The evaluation reports large reductions in video duration sent to MLLMs and improved F1 on cross-camera vehicle matching without MLLM calls. The broader idea is that semantic operators over rich media need database-style logical plans, not just prompt pipelines.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a concrete example of the database community turning foundation-model inference into optimizable query plans.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Context construction is becoming the core systems problem for structured foundation models.** LoGIC, TrajectoryDB, and recent agent-memory work all point to the same shift: once models can ingest structured context, the hard problem becomes selecting, compressing, validating, and versioning the context.

- **Tabular foundation models are being pulled into graph and relational settings.** The interesting question is no longer “can a TFM classify OpenML rows?” but whether TFM-style ICL can work over graph neighborhoods, relational joins, evolving schemas, and constrained deployment budgets.

- **Unlearning and privacy work is becoming modality-specific.** Tabular unlearning now has schema-induced retain/forget conflicts; MoE private training has role-specific gradient/noise issues; agent memory has restore-counterfactual eviction audits. Generic privacy recipes are losing credibility.

- **Generative-model theory is converging around geometry and target steering.** Newton Matching, LATS, diffusion-style smoothing, and recent score/flow work all treat sampling and fine-tuning as geometry over distributions rather than isolated objectives.

- **Database research is absorbing foundation-model inference as query optimization.** Semantic operators, video relational algebra, factorized prompts, Text-to-SQL mutation, and ANN causal diagnosis all suggest a durable systems agenda: make expensive model calls optimizable, inspectable, and composable.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **AhaBench: Do Agents Learn from Prior Experience?** A benchmark for long-horizon continual-learning agents, with puzzle, Euler-style, and vending-agent components plus a scorecard separating initial competence, post-experience score, and learning lift. Worth suppressing because it may become a standard reference for “experience actually helps” evaluations. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Data Scout: Targeted Web Crawling for Domain-Specific Pretraining Corpora.** Instead of filtering CommonCrawl, it uses an LLM-expanded taxonomy, search-query seeds, and a domain classifier to crawl specialized corpora; the paper reports much higher math-content yield and substantial content missing from CommonCrawl. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Distribution-free inference on the number of changepoints / CLOCC.** Rohan Hore and Aaditya Ramdas prove impossibility of nontrivial distribution-free upper confidence bounds for the number of changepoints, then construct a conformal finite-sample lower confidence bound under within-segment exchangeability. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))

- **SmartANN: Object Causal Modeling Boosts Approximate Nearest Neighbor Diagnosis and Auto-Design.** Treats ANN pipelines as ordered causal objects and diagnoses first-failing components before redesigning IVF-PQ/HNSW workflows. This could influence vector-database observability. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **What Eviction Destroys: A Restore-Counterfactual Audit of Forgetting in Agent Memory.** A useful evaluation protocol for separating irreversible memory-eviction losses from recoverable retrieval failures in long-context/agent-memory systems. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))
