## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Do Tabular Foundation Models Still Need Feature Engineering?**  
   **Authors:** Yifan Wu, Pinjun Dong, Jiran Tao, Binyan Jiang  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv, listed September 15, 2026. **Link:** arXiv record in citation.  
   This is the most directly relevant item today: a controlled study asking whether hand-built tabular feature transformations still help as tabular foundation models improve. The headline is nuanced and useful: feature-engineering gains concentrate in earlier TFM generations and become negligible for the strongest models, but related-dataset in-context information still helps. That distinction matters because it shifts the research frontier from “better column transformations” toward “better context acquisition, retrieval, and dataset-level transfer.” It also fits the recent pattern that tabular FMs increasingly behave like context-sensitive learners rather than better encoders of a fixed matrix. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Why you should care:** It is a clean signal that, for strong TFMs, the leverage may be moving from feature engineering to context engineering.

2. **Tabby: An Open Pretraining Recipe for Time Series Foundation Models**  
   **Authors:** Shifeng Xie, Bahaeddine Abdessalem, Zehao Xiao, Youssef Attia El Hili, Ambroise Odonnat, Zhiwei Dong, Lei Zan, Themis Palpanas, Jianfeng Zhang, Lujia Pan, Keli Zhang, Malik Tiomoko  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv technical report, listed September 15, 2026. **Link:** arXiv record in citation.  
   Tabby is a 145M-parameter long-context probabilistic time-series foundation model with an unusually useful “recipe” contribution: pretraining data, synthetic generators, training schedule, intermediate supervision, and open-source release. The corpus mixes extended real-world collections with synthetic dynamics from KernelSynth and CauKerV2, the latter composing temporal dynamics through sampled structural causal models. The model supports contexts up to 8,192 observations and is evaluated across forecasting, classification, and anomaly detection. The important part is not just another TSFM scorecard; it is the reproducible build protocol for studying how synthetic causal temporal data interacts with real pretraining corpora. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Why you should care:** It is a practical bridge between structured synthetic priors, causal time-series simulation, and reusable foundation-model pretraining.

3. **Numerical Stability of Linear Algebra Operations over Relational Databases**  
   **Authors:** Andrei Draghici, Yuchen He, Dan Olteanu  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv cs.DB, listed September 15, 2026. **Link:** arXiv record in citation.  
   This paper opens a surprisingly underdeveloped problem: numerical stability for linear algebra over matrices implicitly defined by relational joins. The key observation is that classical backward stability can be misleading because perturbations in the expanded join matrix may not correspond to any perturbation of the input database. The authors propose projected backward stability for the composition of join query plus numerical operation, and define a database condition number tied to the replication of input values into the join matrix. This is foundational for in-database ML, factorized learning, and any system that wants to push linear algebra through relational structure safely. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))  
   **Why you should care:** It gives a formal language for when “ML over joins” is numerically trustworthy, not merely asymptotically efficient.

4. **Stabilizing Performative Feedback Loops with Minimal Model Deployments**  
   **Authors:** Gabriele Farina, Juan Carlos Perdomo  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv, listed September 15, 2026. **Link:** arXiv record in citation.  
   Performative prediction remains a core theory problem for deployed ML: predictions alter the distribution from which future data are drawn. This paper targets the costly part—learning a performatively stable predictor when each candidate model must be deployed to observe its induced distribution. The contribution is an algorithm that, in the high-accuracy regime, finds a randomized stable predictor with nearly minimal deployments and exponentially fewer deployments than prior approaches, plus a derandomization result under weak performative effects and well-conditioned losses. The technical link to expected variational inequalities is also likely to matter beyond the immediate setting. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Why you should care:** It is a sharp deployment-aware learning result for adaptive systems where experimentation itself changes the population.

5. **Exact Finite Attention Responses From RoPE Derivatives**  
   **Authors:** Julie Huang, Maggie Chlon, Gregory Gutin, Leon Chlon  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv stat.ML/cs.LG, listed September 15, 2026. **Link:** arXiv record in citation.  
   This is a technically interesting interpretability/control paper: it derives exact local responses for attention interventions from RoPE derivatives, allowing candidate edits to be scored from a cached baseline plus one backward pass. The method carries finite positional displacement through softmax without linearizing rotation or normalization, and explicitly captures key–value interaction terms that additive attribution omits. Reported intervention sweeps show large reductions in downstream margin error relative to simpler Jacobian or quadratic approximations, and the calculus yields KL certificates, query-conditioned gradient-step representations, and minimum-norm query controls. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))  
   **Why you should care:** It is the kind of mechanistic calculus that could turn attention analysis from descriptive patching into efficient, certified local control.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Context engineering is replacing feature engineering.** The new TFM feature-engineering study and Tabby’s pretraining recipe both point toward a common pattern: stronger foundation models benefit less from hand-crafted transformations and more from better auxiliary context, synthetic curricula, and retrieved related tasks. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Data systems are becoming semantic control planes for agents.** COMPASS, Chronos, anticipatory databases, TEAR, and recent prior work on agentic transactions all treat schemas, KGs, state branches, and context windows as optimization objects rather than passive storage substrates. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **Evaluation is becoming budget- and deployment-surface aware.** BudgetBench, performative-stability deployment counts, and resource-allocation critiques all reflect a move away from single-number benchmark scores toward capability surfaces over context, cost, information access, latency, and deployment interactions. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Mechanistic interpretability is shifting toward certified intervention prediction.** Exact RoPE response calculus and formal verification of interpretable replacement networks are both attempts to make interpretability outputs decision-relevant under perturbation, not merely plausible on clean examples. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **BudgetBench: A Budget-Tiered Protocol and Pilot Harness for Memory Strategy Evaluation in Local LLM Agents.** A reusable harness that sweeps per-call token budgets from 2K to 32K and reports quality, utilization, latency, and budget-violation rates. Worth suppressing because it is likely to reappear as agent-memory evaluation infrastructure. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **COMPASS: Steering Distributed Vector Search with Scientific Knowledge Graphs.** KG-based sharding and query routing for scientific vector search, searching only 13–18% of the corpus while preserving broadcast recall and improving multi-hop evidence recovery. This is especially relevant to scientific-data lakes and structured retrieval. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **Chronos: Efficient Bolt-on Branching Across Data Stores for Stateful Agentic Applications.** A cross-store branching/merge layer for PostgreSQL, SQLite, DuckDB, Qdrant, and DBMS-backed filesystems; highly relevant to speculative agent execution and reproducible data-agent workflows. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **MUSE-Bench: Beyond Numerical Time Series.** A 14-dataset benchmark for multimodal forecasting with metadata, events, holidays, news, images, and numerical covariates; notable because it tests whether context-aware forecasters actually use context robustly. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **TEAR: Table Extraction with Attribute Recommendation from Texts via LLMs.** A SIGMOD 2027 Round 2 preprint proposing dynamic extraction instructions plus open-ended attribute recommendation for naturally occurring text, with a benchmark and manual annotations. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))
