## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **TabPFN-3.5: Technical Report**  
   **Authors:** Benjamin Jäger, Nick Erickson, Léo Grinsztajn, Felix Birkel, Klemens Flöge, Oscar Key, Jonas Kübler, Gaël Varoquaux collaborators, Bernhard Schölkopf, Yann LeCun, Madelon Hulsebos, Noah Hollmann, Frank Hutter, et al.  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv, new listing on September 17, 2026. **Link:** arXiv.  
   This is the most directly relevant item today: a new flagship Tabular Foundation Model from the Prior Labs / TabPFN ecosystem. The report claims major gains over TabPFN-3 on TabArena, extends the practical envelope to non-i.i.d. temporal/grouped splits, string/text/image columns, high-cardinality categoricals, wide tables, relational tasks, and time-series forecasting. It also introduces TabPFN-3.5-Fast, TabPFN-3.5-Plus, and an upgraded Thinking mode with faster inference-time computation. The breadth matters more than any single benchmark claim: the center of gravity in tabular FM work is shifting from “small clean OpenML tables” toward messy practitioner data. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))  
   **Why you should care:** This is likely to become the reference baseline/release against which new tabular FM, relational FM, and tabular-agent papers will be judged.

2. **Pay Only for Disagreement: Certified No-Regression Verdicts for Model Updates with Matching Label-Complexity Bounds**  
   **Author:** Vishnu Bindu Balachandran  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv, new listing on September 17, 2026. **Link:** arXiv.  
   This paper formalizes model-update promotion as paired risk-difference auditing, exploiting the identity that two models’ risk difference is supported only where their predictions disagree. DISCERN has a zero-label tier for benign low-disagreement updates and a sequential audited tier that labels sampled disagreements using anytime-valid confidence sequences, robust to stopping rules and even adversarial label routing. The theory gives finite-sample validity and matching label-complexity bounds, while the empirical section reports 14,000+ replayed audit streams over 785 update pairs, including LoRA fine-tunes up to 1.4B parameters. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Why you should care:** It is a clean statistical primitive for post-market monitoring of foundation-model updates, quantization changes, vendor swaps, and tabular-FM distillation.

3. **MoRE: Mixture of Reused Experts**  
   **Authors:** Eric S. Qiu, Utku Umur Acikalin, Justin Lovelace, Christian Belardi, Arjun B. Mulchandani, Carla P. Gomes, Kilian Q. Weinberger  
   **Venue/source/date:** COLM 2026 / arXiv, new listing on September 17, 2026. **Link:** arXiv.  
   MoRE combines two scaling ideas that have mostly been studied separately: sparse expert capacity and recurrent/weight-shared depth. Instead of giving each layer its own expert set, adjacent layers share expert pools, while layer-specific routers and lightweight depth embeddings let the same experts play different roles at different depths. The paper reports experiments from 114M to 1.15B parameters, claiming better perplexity and downstream performance than standard MoEs and weight-sharing baselines at matched compute and parameter budgets. The idea is simple enough to be widely tested, and it intersects with current evidence that sparse MoEs are brittle under data repetition. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Why you should care:** Shared expert pools may be an important middle ground between dense depth, looped transformers, and parameter-heavy MoEs.

4. **Preventing Model Collapse: A Fisher-Rao Perspective on the Dynamics of Training with Synthetic Data**  
   **Authors:** Matteo Marchi, João Pedro Silvestre, Bahman Gharesifard, Paulo Tabuada  
   **Venue/source/date:** Extended version of CDC 2026 paper / arXiv, new listing on September 17, 2026. **Link:** arXiv.  
   This theory paper revisits recursive synthetic-data training through information geometry. Prior lower bounds on the human-to-synthetic data ratio can become vacuous in high-dimensional categorical spaces because they rely on Euclidean geometry. The authors instead analyze training dynamics on the probability simplex under the Fisher-Rao metric, deriving contraction and invariance bounds that remain meaningful as dimension grows. The paper’s practical relevance is obvious: synthetic data is now a routine part of LLM, tabular, time-series, and agent training pipelines, but the stability conditions for recursive generation remain poorly understood. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Why you should care:** It gives a more plausible mathematical language for data-mixture stability than Euclidean distribution drift.

5. **Provable Guarantees and Efficient Learning of Structural Equation Models with Latent Confounders**  
   **Authors:** Weijian Yu, Jean Honorio  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv, new listing on September 17, 2026. **Link:** arXiv.  
   The paper studies linear SEM causal discovery when latent confounders induce spurious dependencies among observed variables. The algorithm recovers the observed-variable precision matrix as sparse plus low-rank: sparse structure captures conditional dependencies among observables, while low-rank structure captures a small number of latent confounders. It then iteratively identifies terminal observed nodes and reconstructs the directed graph. The guarantee scales as roughly `max{s log p, r p}` samples for `p` observed variables, `r` confounders, and `s` edges. This is a useful bridge between causal discovery, graphical-model decomposition, and practical latent-confounding diagnostics. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Why you should care:** Sparse-plus-low-rank causal discovery remains one of the few tractable routes for moving beyond causal sufficiency assumptions.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Tabular foundation models are moving from benchmark dominance to product-surface pressure.** TabPFN-3.5’s emphasis on non-i.i.d. splits, text/date/high-cardinality features, multimodality, relational tasks, time series, fast variants, and thinking mode shows the field is now optimizing for messy deployment, not just OpenML leaderboard wins.

- **Evaluation is becoming label-budgeted and certificate-driven.** DISCERN, selective-prediction label-budget work, pass@k identifiability work from earlier this week, and conformal/policy-safety papers all point toward a common theme: “good evaluation” is now an adaptive statistical design problem.

- **Synthetic data stability is getting more geometric.** Fisher-Rao analysis of model collapse complements recent diffusion information-geometry and data-mixture work: researchers are searching for geometry that respects probability-simplex structure rather than Euclidean distance between high-dimensional counts.

- **Architectural efficiency is converging on reuse.** MoRE, looped transformers, recurrent SSM analyses, shared experts, KV-cache read-depth methods, and low-rank adaptation papers all ask the same question: what can be reused across depth, time, experts, or context without losing specialization?

- **Causal ML keeps relaxing unrealistic observability assumptions.** Today’s latent-confounded SEM paper fits a broader month-long wave: unstructured treatments/outcomes, transportability, interference, irregular histories, longitudinal functional representations, and conformal safe policies.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **Locating Hidden Failures Makes Long-Horizon Agents More Reliable / Traverse.** A large annotated failure corpus: 2,518 agent trajectories, 6,967 mistakes, 78 failure types, plus a trained 4B verifier called Scout. The key point is that final task success hides harmful intermediate failures, and frontier judges locate first mistakes poorly. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Where Grokking Happens: Distributed Utility and Fourier Recoding Without a Module Switch.** Uses behavior-aligned activation games to argue grokking is not a clean “MLP memorizes, attention generalizes” switch, but spectral recoding of an existing distributed circuit. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Fathom: Per-Query Read Depth for Sparse Decoding over Offloaded KV Caches.** A systems idea for million-token agent sessions: each query decides how many quantized key bits to read from host memory, reducing scan traffic while preserving top-k behavior in reported settings. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Principled Koopman Representations with Kalman Inference for Efficient Time-Series Prediction.** K²SVD learns leading singular functions of the Koopman operator, then performs Kalman inference in a compact latent state-space model; relevant to representation learning for dynamical systems. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **iMINDBench: iEEG Multi-Institution Neural Decoding Benchmark.** A 15-task, three-dataset benchmark for neural decoding and foundation-model evaluation under preprocessing and cross-institution generalization constraints. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))
