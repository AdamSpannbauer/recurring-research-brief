## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **BI-Agent and BI-Bench: Towards Automating End-to-End Business Intelligence**  
   **Authors:** Chuxuan Hu, Yeye He, Penny Zhou, Wee Hyong Tok, Daniel Kang, Surajit Chaudhuri  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv, released September 21, 2026. **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   This is the strongest new structured-data item: a benchmark and agent framework for end-to-end business intelligence, where the system must identify tables, transform data, build joins, and answer user-facing dashboard questions rather than merely solve isolated text-to-SQL tasks. The benchmark is built from real public BI projects and dashboard-derived questions; frontier LLMs reportedly remain below 50% accuracy. BI-Agent decomposes the workflow into search, join, transform, and analysis subtasks, and uses synthesized trajectories for SFT/RL post-training, yielding large accuracy gains.  
   **Why you should care:** It reframes “data agents” around the real bottleneck Adam tracks: data preparation and schema/join reasoning before SQL or analysis ever starts.

2. **Sparse Priors for Efficient Distribution Learning**  
   **Authors:** Saumya Goyal, Barnabás Póczos  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv, released September 21, 2026. **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   This theory paper proposes “sparse priors” and a corresponding “sparse dimension” to explain when generative distribution learning can avoid the usual curse-of-dimensionality sample rates. The authors prove Bayesian risk lower bounds of order \(\Omega(\sqrt{k/n})\) for \(k\)-sparse priors and matching-up-to-logs upper bounds for total variation under additional assumptions, then connect distribution learning to learning-to-sample in the Bayesian setting. The framing is attractive because it tries to formalize the implicit structural priors behind modern generative modeling rather than only assume smoothness.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a clean route toward theory for why synthetic-data and generative models can work in high-dimensional but structured regimes.

3. **Stiefel-AdamW: Geometry-Aware AdamW for Linear Factorization Blocks**  
   **Authors:** Emanuele Zangrando, Marco Sutti, Francesco Tudisco  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv, released September 21, 2026. **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   Low-rank blocks such as LoRA adapters, compressed linear layers, and query-key factorizations have gauge symmetries that ordinary AdamW ignores. This paper constrains one factor to the Stiefel manifold while leaving the other Euclidean, reducing noncompact scaling degeneracy while retaining AdamW-style diagonal preconditioning. The authors claim convergence guarantees and practical gains on GPT-2, ViT, Mistral-7B LoRA-style fine-tuning, and GPT-2 pretraining, with minimal overhead. It fits a growing line of work arguing that optimizer geometry, not just rank or parameter count, governs adaptation quality.  
   **Why you should care:** It is directly relevant to recent LoRA-rank and Muon/Adam geometry results: factorization gauge choice is becoming a first-class optimization design variable.

4. **The Stochastic Shift: A New Evaluation Paradigm for Text-to-SQL with AI Operators**  
   **Authors:** Tarfah Alrashed, Fatma Ozcan, Per Jacobsson, Tal Neiman, Xianshun Chen  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv, released September 21, 2026. **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))  
   As SQL systems incorporate AI operators over text, images, and other unstructured values, exact result matching becomes the wrong evaluation primitive. This paper formalizes the evaluation failure modes created by stochastic/flexible AI operators and proposes a multilayer framework that separates deterministic relational correctness from AI-semantic correctness. In experiments spanning BigQuery and ThalamusDB, traditional execution accuracy badly undercounts correct translations, while the proposed decomposition reaches much higher reported accuracy.  
   **Why you should care:** This is a likely benchmark-design inflection point for semantic SQL, AI-native databases, and hybrid structured/unstructured query evaluation.

5. **Diagonalized Attention for Individualized Regression: Latent-Row Localization and Prediction**  
   **Authors:** Borui Peng, Liwei Lin, Feifei Wang, Long Feng  
   **Venue/source/date:** arXiv, released September 21, 2026. **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))  
   This statistical paper studies matrix-valued covariates—tokens, patches, rows—where each observation has its own sparse rows of predictive signal but effects are shared population-wide. The proposed diagonalized attention model uses query-key scores to localize sample-specific rows and a value matrix for regression, with parameter dimension independent of sample size. The theory gives recovery and prediction guarantees under score-separation and concentration assumptions, providing a statistical explanation for attention as sample-adaptive sparse localization.  
   **Why you should care:** It offers a useful formal bridge between attention mechanisms, individualized feature selection, and interpretable representation learning for heterogeneous structured inputs.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Structured-data agents are moving from SQL generation to workflow execution.** BI-Agent, AI-operator SQL evaluation, COAL-SQL, and database-agent failure corpora all point to a shift from “generate a query” toward search, join discovery, transformation, verification, and post-training on tool trajectories.

- **Evaluation is becoming semantic and decomposed.** Exact-match execution accuracy breaks for AI operators; pass/fail endpoint metrics hide intermediate database-agent failures; and precision changes can alter greedy decoding. The community is converging on layered evaluation: relational logic, semantic operators, tool transport, and model-inference determinism need separate checks.

- **Optimizer/factorization geometry remains hot.** Stiefel-AdamW sits alongside recent Muon, LoRA-rank, ISO-LoRA, and factorization-gauge work: modern adaptation quality increasingly depends on the geometry induced by parameterization and optimizer, not only the nominal architecture.

- **Attention theory is becoming more statistical.** Diagonalized attention and softmax-abstention/noise-filtering papers suggest a more granular view of attention as localization, filtering, abstention, and sample-specific signal extraction rather than a monolithic “context mixing” primitive.

- **Generative modeling theory is pivoting from smoothness to structure.** Sparse priors and discrete-diffusion scheduling analyses reflect a broader move toward explaining when generative models escape worst-case dimensionality through priors, dependence profiles, or schedule design.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **COAL-SQL: Coverage-Guided Augmentation and Failure-Driven Learning for Text-to-SQL Post-Training.** A compact post-training recipe for text-to-SQL that targets structural gaps and accumulated failures using coverage-guided augmentation plus GRPO/SFT repair; worth suppressing because it may reappear as code or benchmark chatter. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **What Stops a Small Language Model From Driving a Database Agent.** A released corpus/scorer/verifier from 8,199 production-style local-model database-agent runs, arguing many failures are tool transport and server-interface issues rather than pure reasoning failures. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **Schedule optimization for tau-leaping in masked discrete diffusion.** A useful theory artifact for discrete diffusion: the paper derives how dependence-density profiles determine the optimal schedule and factorization error for block-reveal samplers. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))

- **Scaling Discovery through Test-Time Communication.** Multi-agent communication reportedly beats independent best-of-\(k\) attempts on ARC-AGI-3 and research-style tasks when sufficient compute and progress feedback exist; interesting as a possible evaluation artifact for collaborative agentic discovery. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **kgsteward.** A pragmatic scientific-data-management tool for building and maintaining distributed RDF knowledge graphs from version-controlled configuration, with SPARQL validation queries that double as usage examples for humans and AI agents. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))
