## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Robust Hierarchical Structures for Agentic Document Analysis** — Ruiying Ma, Yiming Lin, Aditya G. Parameswaran. **Source/Venue:** arXiv; to appear in **PACMMOD / SIGMOD 2027**. **Release date:** submitted September 27, 2026. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33322))  
   This is the strongest data-management item in the last-day window. The paper observes that LLM document agents usually flatten PDFs/Word files into plain text, throwing away section/subsection structure that could sharply reduce cost and improve grounding. The proposed SHED workflow targets a robust-and-compact hierarchy: every inferred section should safely contain the true section text, while avoiding needless over-inclusion. The paper gives formal document classes for robustness guarantees, then reports 13%–68% F1 gains over non-LLM baselines and 9%–15% over expensive LLM approaches, with downstream agent accuracy gains and up to 10× cost reduction.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a clean example of database-style guarantees becoming the missing substrate for reliable LLM agents.

2. **FOCUS: Fixed-Confidence Online Causal Learning Using Sequential Adaptive Interventions** — Haijie Xu, Chen Zhang. **Source/Venue:** arXiv. **Release date:** submitted September 26, 2026. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32165))  
   FOCUS tackles a crisp causal-learning setting: recover a linear-Gaussian causal DAG and edge weights starting from zero observational data, choosing interventions sequentially until a fixed-confidence guarantee is met. The contribution is both algorithmic and information-theoretic: an instance-dependent lower bound, a max–min intervention allocation game, and a computable concentration inequality for accumulated KL divergence when causal parameters are shared across interventions. The expected stopping time matches the lower bound in its log-confidence dependence, up to an instance-dependent constant.  
   **Why you should care:** It pushes causal discovery toward active experimental design with explicit sample-complexity accounting rather than passive graph recovery.

3. **WeaveData: A Multimodal Data Analysis System with Self-Critiquing and Self-Evolving LLM Plans** — Min Jia, Shihao Zhou, Jun-Peng Zhu, Peng Cai, Kai Xu, Chao Zhang, Li Li, Aoying Zhou, Heng Long, Qiu Cui, Liu Tang, Qi Liu. **Source/Venue:** arXiv. **Release date:** submitted September 28, 2026. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34764))  
   WeaveData is a compact but timely system paper for natural-language analysis over relational tables, text, and images. Its core move is to make LLM-generated analysis plans typed, inspectable, and self-repairing: plans are critiqued before execution, outputs are checked afterward against the question, and failed plans are evolved using actual data while preserving still-valid intermediate results. The system also grounds planning in a metadata knowledge graph, asks clarifying questions, and records evidence in an interactive notebook.  
   **Why you should care:** It joins the recent “semantic query plan” wave, but emphasizes plan evolution and evidence-backed notebook interaction.

4. **Agnostic Smoothed Online Regression with Adversarial Responses** — Xuanyu Chen, Yue Yu. **Source/Venue:** arXiv. **Release date:** submitted September 26, 2026. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32478))  
   This theory paper studies smoothed online prediction, a regime between i.i.d. sampling and fully adversarial covariates, now with bounded adversarial responses. The Hedge-Cover algorithm achieves sublinear regret scaling with pseudo-dimension, the covariate smoothness parameter, and horizon, using a prior tied to the number of disagreements between a consistent selector and target function. The paper says this resolves an open problem on minimax-optimal adaptive regret for agnostic smoothed online regression and gives matching lower bounds for linear predictors.  
   **Why you should care:** Smoothed/adaptive adversaries are increasingly relevant for deployed ML systems, and this gives a sharp regret template.

5. **Continuous-Time Trajectory Generation from Discrete Observations with Stochasticity** — Ruifeng Shang, Shu Liu, Yuhua Zhu. **Source/Venue:** arXiv. **Release date:** submitted September 25, 2026. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32026))  
   PhiBE-Flow targets stochastic physical systems observed only at discrete times, where learning one-step transitions can miss the continuous-time probability evolution. The method directly estimates the probability velocity field induced by the underlying SDE, without requiring known SDE coefficients or score estimation. The paper gives convergence guarantees that account for time discretization and finite samples, then evaluates on stochastic systems, Navier–Stokes dynamics, and video generation.  
   **Why you should care:** It is another sign that flow/trajectory methods are becoming a bridge between generative modeling, scientific simulation, and Bayesian filtering.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Agentic data systems are converging on typed, inspectable plans.** SHED, WeaveData, KathDB-FAO, Concord, BI-Agent, and related systems are all moving away from raw prompting toward typed plans, provenance, critique, repair, and metadata graphs.

- **Causal learning is becoming more experimental-design flavored.** FOCUS, recent causal tabular pretraining, conformal policy learning, and causal interactive learning all emphasize what data or intervention should be acquired next, not just how to estimate from fixed data.

- **Flow methods are spreading into scientific dynamics and sampling reliability.** PhiBE-Flow, NF²M, AECSF, and recent Wasserstein/flow-matching papers show a broad push toward statistically controlled continuous-time generation and filtering.

- **Evaluation is shifting from scalar accuracy to structural validity.** Text-to-SQL with stochastic AI operators, document hierarchy robustness, agent trace auditing, and TFM attention/conditional-density benchmarks all ask whether the computation itself is valid, not merely whether the final answer matches.

- **Venue activity remains unusually continuous.** TMLR’s September page is still changing daily, while NeurIPS acceptances are partially visible only through fragmented sources; duplicate suppression should remain conservative for anything marked “accepted” but not yet in stable proceedings.

- **Bridging Stochastic Flow Maps and Boltzmann Generators with Normalizing Flows** — NF²M combines stochastic flow maps with exact-likelihood conditional normalizing flows, reweighting denoising transitions during generation rather than only at the end; worth tracking for simulation-based ML. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))
