## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Seldon TFM family: Seldon-Mini, Seldon and Seldon+**  
   **Authors:** Stanislas Angebault, Nicolas Atienza, Nicola Cancedda, Aziz Fouché, Salomé Gobbi, Ekaterina Iakovleva, Nicolas Makaroff, Arthur Nahmias, Paul Novello, Alexandre Pasquiou  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** November 24, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv:2611.32257.  
   Seldon is another serious entry in the now-crowded open tabular foundation model race. The paper reports three models: Seldon-Mini, Seldon, and the classifier Seldon+, with Seldon+ ranking first on the authors’ 124-dataset TabBench benchmark and missing-value variant; the open-weight 27M and 213M models are reported on the TabArena speed/accuracy Pareto frontier, trained only on synthetic tables, and architecturally close to Kumo Tabular, TabFM, and TabPFN-3.5. The importance is less any single leaderboard claim than the convergence of recipes: synthetic-only pretraining, ICL transformers, increasingly open checkpoints, and benchmark proliferation. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32257))  
   **Why you should care:** Seldon strengthens the signal that tabular FMs are becoming a reproducible model class rather than isolated lab artifacts.

2. **CaLA: Causal Latent Adjustment for World Models under Adaptive Policies**  
   **Authors:** Robert Ness, Sofiane Ennadir, Oleg Smirnov  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** November 23, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv:2611.31431.  
   CaLA targets a subtle but important failure mode in learned world models: imagined rollouts trained from adaptive-policy logs can inherit treatment-outcome confounding. The paper builds a fully specified Match-3 structural causal model where latent player skill affects both assigned difficulty and churn, then shows that unadjusted logged-trajectory models can recommend harmful difficulty settings. CaLA adds a DAG-constrained latent player representation inferred from pretreatment history, improving population-level intervention estimation and competitive individualized decisions. The paper’s key contribution is conceptual discipline: predictive fit, residual correction, and causal decision quality are not interchangeable. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31431))  
   **Why you should care:** This is a clean warning for model-based RL, recommender simulators, and “digital twin” work using adaptively collected logs.

3. **Transformers with Signatures: Towards Universal In-Context Learning in Path Space**  
   **Authors:** Ziheng Cheng, Xin Guo, Ben Hambly  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** November 23, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv:2611.31439.  
   This theory paper extends in-context learning analysis from IID demonstrations to path-valued, dependent data by combining transformers with path-signature features. The authors construct a causal softmax transformer with two attention heads per layer that emulates in-context Lasso over signature features, then derive finite-sample prediction bounds under sparse signature approximation and moment/concentration assumptions. Experiments cover sparse recovery, nonlinear path regression, execution-cost prediction, and financial payoff inference, where signature-based ICL reportedly matches or beats vanilla transformer baselines with fewer trainable parameters. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31439))  
   **Why you should care:** It gives a mathematically natural bridge between sequence/path statistics and transformer ICL theory—relevant to time series, finance, event logs, and continuous-time data.

4. **U-Statistics-Powered LLM Reasoning**  
   **Authors:** Shijin Gong, Kai Ye, Victor Chernozhukov, Xinyu Zhang, Chengchun Shi  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** November 24, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv:2611.33064.  
   This paper treats a core engineering choice in GRPO-style reasoning-model training—the group size—as a statistical estimation problem. By exploiting the U-statistic structure of the GRPO policy gradient, the authors recast group-size selection as variance-ratio estimation and propose a pessimistic estimator for unstable denominator regimes. Empirically, a jackknife-based pessimistic rule on synthetic Reasoning Gym tasks and real math/code benchmarks reduces policy-gradient MSE and improves policy optimization relative to fixed group sizes, with modest extra compute. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33064))  
   **Why you should care:** It is exactly the kind of statistical reframing that could turn RL-for-reasoning from cookbook hyperparameter search into estimable procedure design.

5. **Meta-Amortized Cognitive Modeling**  
   **Authors:** Jerry M. Huang, Lukas Schumacher, Niek Stevenson, Mischa von Krause, Stefan T. Radev  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** November 24, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv:2611.31914.  
   CogFormerV2 generalizes amortized Bayesian inference from a single simulator/model to a large space of cognitive models. The paper reports one network trained in about a day on one GPU that supports posterior inference across more than 14.6 billion model configurations spanning binary, multi-alternative, and continuous decisions, changing covariates, parameter sets, trial counts, and contamination levels. It is also released as an open-source interactive application. For Adam’s interests, the important idea is reusable inference amortization over a structured model class, not cognitive modeling per se. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31914))  
   **Why you should care:** This is a strong example of “foundation-model-like” amortization for Bayesian workflows and could inspire similar meta-inference systems for causal, tabular, and simulation-based models.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Open tabular FM competition is accelerating.** Kumo, TabPFN-3.5, Causilo, WeightedPFN, and now Seldon suggest that tabular FMs are shifting from isolated research artifacts to an open model zoo, with benchmark design, latency, missingness, weighting, and reliability increasingly central. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32257))
- **Causal validity is being pushed into world models and decision simulators.** CaLA highlights that adaptive logs can make world models optimize the wrong intervention, a theme that aligns with recent work on causal foundation models, policy-shift conformal calibration, and causal data fusion. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31431))
- **In-context learning theory is expanding beyond IID tables.** Signature-transformer ICL for path space and Seldon-style tabular ICL point toward a broader theory of context as structured sample, trajectory, or database state—not just prompt examples. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31439))
- **Post-training is being statistically instrumented.** U-statistic analysis of GRPO and clipping analysis for zeroth-order optimization are part of a broader move from RL/post-training heuristics toward estimators, variance diagnostics, and sample-allocation rules. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33064))
- **Databases are reappearing as the AI reliability substrate.** Semantic views, tag reuse, SQL equivalence proofs, vector-search correctness, and alert-preserving compression all reflect a systems response to stochastic model behavior: cache, certify, replay, and verify. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32894))

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **Local subspace encoding for spectral prediction.** LVSE improves raw-spectrum TabPFN on 61 numerical spectral tasks and 76 classification datasets, and also helps classical PLS/Ridge in many cases; useful for thinking about domain-specific representation interfaces for TFMs. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31245))
- **Joint Classification and Generation on Tabular Data.** An empirical comparison of joint tabular classifier/generator designs finds joint models rarely dominate separate classifier-generator pairs but can be close enough to reduce operational complexity. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32225))
- **KuroANN.** A filtered vector-search system that uses match count to avoid unnecessary SSD reads, reporting up to 34.5× over PipeANN-Filter and 9.10× over Faiss in high-recall filtered search. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32908))
- **Alert-Preserving Lossy Compression of Monitoring Time Series.** A correctness-by-alert-state compression scheme for Prometheus-style monitoring rules; attractive as a database-native example of preserving downstream decision semantics rather than raw signal fidelity. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32894))
- **O2N2.** A privacy-preserving ANN design with double obliviousness via randomly rotated kd-tree forests and ORAM, reporting 9.8–125.9× speedups over doubly oblivious baselines. ([arxiv.cnoctave.top](https://arxiv.cnoctave.top/abs/2611.03415?utm_source=openai))
