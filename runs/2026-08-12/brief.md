## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Optimal Inference with Black-box Predictions**  
   **Authors:** Lucas Kania, Abhinav Chakraborty, Edward Kennedy, Larry Wasserman, Sivaraman Balakrishnan  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv, submitted August 10, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10155))  
   This is the cleanest statistical paper in the window: it asks how to use arbitrary black-box predictions inside valid hypothesis tests, rather than treating prediction-assisted inference as a collection of tricks. In a high-dimensional Gaussian sequence model, the authors characterize information-theoretic limits when prediction accuracies are known, and adaptive procedures for orthogonal predictions when accuracies are unknown. The key contribution is a principle for when predictions add inferential power without invalidating tests—highly relevant as ML predictions become nuisance objects, priors, covariates, or synthetic side information in downstream scientific inference.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a serious attempt to put “use model predictions in inference” on an optimality footing, with Kennedy/Wasserman/Balakrishnan authorship as a strong signal.

2. **COMPACT: Spectral Adjustment Scores from a Complete and Irreducible Causal Criterion**  
   **Authors:** Eric V. Strobl  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv, submitted August 10, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10305))  
   COMPACT tackles a practical but under-theorized causal problem: how to reduce high-dimensional baseline covariates to an adjustment score when the analyst does not know which variables belong in the adjustment set. Rather than relying on propensity-score coarseness alone, the paper studies causal graphs over a candidate score, treatment, and outcome—allowing latent variables—and identifies dependence relations invariant to whether treatment causes outcome. The resulting spectral score targets a span combining balancing and outcome-guided coordinates, with proxy recovery, error characterization, bootstrap validity, simulations, and a real-data example.  
   **Why you should care:** It reframes adjustment-score construction as a causal-graph specificity problem, not merely a predictive balancing problem.

3. **Iterative Erasure Count Is Not an Affine-Invariant Concept Dimension**  
   **Authors:** Tingan Jin, Shuhang Dong, Haosong Li, Chung-Hsien Chou  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv, submitted August 11, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record, with ancillary artifact files. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10566))  
   This paper is a useful corrective for representation interpretability. It shows that iterative erasure counts—the number of probe directions removed before a concept seems gone—are not intrinsic concept dimensions: invertible, information-preserving reparameterizations can change the count while preserving the underlying prediction problem. The paper distinguishes model-defined quantities from procedure-defined quantities, gives Gaussian constructions, analyzes QR-style procedures, and stress-tests frozen V-JEPA2 features. The important point is methodological: many “concept dimension” claims are actually claims about a probe, metric, regularizer, optimizer, and coordinate system.  
   **Why you should care:** It sharpens what mechanistic and representation-analysis papers are allowed to claim from erasure-style interventions.

4. **Do Time-Series Forecasters Use the Right History: Recoverability, Recovery, and Functional Use of Temporal Delays**  
   **Authors:** Qipeng Qian, Yuntao Qian  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv, submitted August 11, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10433))  
   The paper separates three often-confounded questions in time-series modeling: whether the true delay is statistically recoverable, whether a model reports that delay, and whether the model functionally uses it. The unsettling result is that forecast risk and even correct delay reporting can coexist with reliance on the wrong lag. In their point-delay tests, a large fraction of N-HiTS and TCN forecasts with correct delay reports and near-oracle risk fail a matched masking test for functional use. A hard routing mechanism through the reported history repairs alignment.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a compact example of evaluation moving from performance to mechanistic faithfulness for structured temporal models.

5. **AI Query Compilation for Unified and Optimized Execution**  
   **Authors:** Yeounoh Chung, Helena Caminal, Fatma Ozcan  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv / PVLDB-style vision paper, submitted August 10, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv record. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10139))  
   This database-systems vision paper proposes compiling hybrid AI queries—ordinary SQL plus LLM inference layers—into a single tensor compute graph. The motivation is that current semantic-query systems suffer from execution-boundary costs: relational operators, model calls, data transfers, and scheduling are optimized in separate layers. Their prototype results on SemBench Reviews and Movies report up to 5.3× latency and 9.8× throughput improvements on TPUs, and the paper lays out open challenges around global compiler optimization, sharding, and query semantics.  
   **Why you should care:** It points toward a plausible next systems layer for semantic operators: not orchestration around models, but compilation through them.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Inference is absorbing predictions, not just evaluating them.** Prediction-assisted inference, COMPACT-style adjustment scores, and reward-SNR acquisition limits all treat ML outputs as statistical objects whose validity depends on identifiability, alignment, or signal-to-noise—not just accuracy.

- **Representation interpretability is becoming measurement-theoretic.** The erasure-count paper and recent TMLR concept-bottleneck work both push against naive scalar interpretability scores. The recurring question is: what is invariant under representation reparameterization, and what is an artifact of the probe/metric/procedure?

- **Temporal and structured models are being audited for functional use.** The time-series delay paper is part of a broader shift from “does the model forecast?” to “does it use the hypothesized causal/temporal/relational mechanism?”

- **Semantic data systems are moving from agentic orchestration to compilation.** AI Query Compilation and recent semantic-operator systems suggest the database community is converging on execution plans where LLM calls become optimizable operators, not opaque services.

- **Agent evaluation is shifting toward actionability thresholds.** The reward-SNR floor paper and TMLR’s tool-agent verification items both ask when a model-derived signal can support a deployable decision policy, not merely a retrospective average gain.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **Sheaf-Based Federated Representation Learning** — a geometric representation-learning framework for heterogeneous federated systems that avoids assuming a shared global latent space, using learnable sheaf restriction maps and Procrustes updates. Worth watching if federated/multi-institutional structured data representation learning becomes central. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10016))

- **Detecting an Effect Is Not Learning to Act on It** — introduces a reward-SNR floor for deciding when LLM-derived auxiliary observations can support per-instance acquisition policies. The idea is broadly relevant to “should I call a slow model / oracle / feature extractor?” decisions in data agents and recommenders. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10441))

- **UserToolBench** — a benchmark for personalized decision making in tool-use LLMs with hidden user profiles, 36 tool sets, 170 tools, and multi-turn trajectories. It looks less central than CausalDS/DataAgent-Bench, but it targets delegation under latent preferences, which will matter for data-assistant personalization. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **CurveFP** — rational-radix logarithmic datatypes with closed products for LLM training/inference. The arithmetic-co-design angle is interesting if low-precision formats become more architecture-aware rather than scalar-fidelity-only. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **On the Statistical Limits of Self-Improving Agents** — newly visible in TMLR’s August list; details need a closer read, but the title and placement make it worth suppressing and revisiting if the OpenReview discussion or artifact becomes influential. ([jmlr.org](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/papers/))
