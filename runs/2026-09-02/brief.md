## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Context Window Failures in Relational Foundation Models**  
   **Authors:** Denis Oliveira Correa, Francisco Galuppo Azevedo  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv; accepted at the 2nd ICML 2026 Workshop on Foundation Models for Structured Data  
   **Release date:** September 2, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   This is the most directly relevant item today: a small but sharp negative result for relational foundation models. The authors introduce **Animus**, a synthetic financial relational dataset where income prediction requires aggregating over very high-cardinality transaction histories. Recent relational deep-learning / relational-foundation-model architectures are bottlenecked by neighborhood budgets and row truncation: RT, Griffin, and RelGT all stay at weak raw-representation performance, while a conventional temporal pre-aggregation recovers much stronger signal. The paper is valuable less as a benchmark leaderboard item than as a failure-mode probe: it asks whether current RDL architectures have silently inherited context-window pathologies from LLMs.  
   **Why you should care:** This is a clean reminder that “foundation model for relational data” is not solved if the model cannot represent the aggregation statistics any competent feature engineer would compute.

2. **SMELT: Scaling Laws for Compute-Matched MoE Looped Transformers**  
   **Authors:** Shaowen Wang, Ge Zhang, Kairong Luo, Yuhao Wu, Shaofan Liu, Jiaheng Liu, Wenhao Huang, Shen Yan, Jian Li  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** September 2, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   SMELT studies looped Transformers under a much cleaner comparison than many recurrence/depth-reuse papers: per-token FLOPs, total non-embedding parameters, and KV cache are matched against an unlooped MoE baseline. The recipe loops the middle half of layers twice and scales to 54B non-embedding parameters. The reported Chinchilla-style scaling laws suggest 6.8–18.0% training-FLOP savings on the compute-optimal frontier, with gains strongest in code, long samples, and ICL-heavy settings. The mechanistic story—second visits reduce attention sinks and redirect mass toward content tokens—is preliminary but plausible.  
   **Why you should care:** If robust, this is a practical scaling-law result for depth reuse rather than another small-model recurrence curiosity.

3. **Counterfactual Fragility Certificates: Exposing High-Confidence Brittleness under Structured Evidence Failure**  
   **Authors:** Filippo Cenacchi, Longbing Cao, Runze Yang  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** September 2, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   This paper proposes **Counterfactual Fragility Certificates** for tabular decision systems: recomputable audit objects describing how a prediction loses support when feature families become unavailable, stale, noisy, delayed, or low-trust. Rather than another uncertainty score or attribution map, the certificate records an ordered evidence-failure trajectory: flip budget, normalized margin-collapse area, thresholds, and a fragility dominance score. Across seven tabular benchmarks and multiple model families, CFC-FDS reportedly detects brittle high-confidence cases far better than non-certificate scores, and under a 20% review budget captures many more brittle cases than confidence or energy scores.  
   **Why you should care:** This is a useful reliability primitive for tabular ML deployments, especially where “what if this evidence source fails?” is more actionable than generic calibration.

4. **Bandits in Prod: Hyperparameter Optimization at Inference Time**  
   **Authors:** Louis Abraham, Tuan-Anh Nguyen, Nicolas Devatine  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** September 2, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   The paper formalizes **online hyperparameter optimization** for production systems whose configurations can only be evaluated through live, noisy feedback—think model routing, retrieval depth, decoding settings, or agent prompting. It casts the problem as an infinitely many-armed bandit over mixed and conditional search spaces. The proposed IMABO framework combines a policy over sampled configurations with an oracle for proposing new ones; IMOSS grows the active set restart-free and comes with quantile-regret bounds. Notably, one oracle is a pretrained tabular foundation model, linking the paper to structured-data priors for black-box systems control.  
   **Why you should care:** This is a natural formalization of a growing operational reality: inference-time ML systems increasingly need safe online experimentation, not static validation-set tuning.

5. **Exact Global MCMC with Denoising Diffusion**  
   **Authors:** Mitch Hill  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** September 2, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))  
   This paper uses denoising diffusion models as global proposal mechanisms for exact MCMC over complex high-dimensional target densities. The key observation is that a forward-plus-reverse diffusion path can be embedded in a Markov chain, but exactness requires a Metropolis–Hastings correction using forward and reverse path densities under a discrete-time SDE approximation. The resulting **Denoising Diffusion Monte Carlo** trains denoisers on locally convergent MALA samples, then combines global diffusion proposals with local MALA. The experiments are preliminary, but the bridge between scalable diffusion training and exact sampling from unnormalized targets is conceptually important.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a promising route to make diffusion-style global moves compatible with Bayesian computation rather than just heuristic generative sampling.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Relational foundation models are entering their “failure taxonomy” phase.** Recent work has moved from “can we make a PFN/Transformer work on multi-table data?” to stress tests around context windows, high-cardinality relations, embedding fusion, aggregation bottlenecks, and leakage between symbolic and neural representations.

- **Reliability artifacts are becoming more protocol-shaped.** Counterfactual fragility certificates, SQL aggregate oracles, selective-risk ladders, verifiable provenance, runtime alarms, and conformal/replicability work all reflect a shift from scalar metrics toward reproducible audit objects.

- **Inference-time systems are being formalized as online decision problems.** Bandits, routing, cascades, context assembly, KV/cache policies, and model selection are converging around the same question: how should a deployed compound AI system learn from live feedback under cost and quality constraints?

- **Diffusion theory is broadening into computation, not only generation.** Recent papers treat diffusion as posterior sampler, exact MCMC proposal, score-difference estimator, discrete diffusion information geometry, and loss-floor object. The generative-model community is rediscovering classical statistical-computation concerns.

- **Depth reuse and recurrence are now scaling-law questions.** SMELT joins recent looped/recurrent Transformer work by insisting on budget-matched comparisons and by probing when repeated layers create a useful computational prior rather than just extra hidden FLOPs.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **Scaled Idempotence in Transformer Attention: Paired OV Geometry and Shared-Value Algebras** — Jiming Feng and Junliang Li report that a sparse subset of effective OV operators nearly close under composition, with evidence across several pretrained MHA/GQA models. Worth watching as a mechanistic-interpretability object linking head geometry to local operator algebras. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Time-Decayed Vector Search in the Rhythm of TANGO** — Jiuqi Wei, Qiyao Luo, Quanqing Xu, Chuanhui Yang, and Themis Palpanas formalize vector search where temporal freshness is part of relevance, then propose score-preserving temporal reductions and a hierarchical graph index. Useful for retrieval systems over evolving corpora. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **Bounded, Indeterminate, or a Bug: A Condition-Aware Oracle for Differential Testing of SQL Aggregates** — Madhulatha Mandarapu and Sandeep Kunkunuru give a condition-aware floating-point oracle for SQL aggregate testing, distinguishing true bugs from legitimate rounding ambiguity. This is a good example of “verification with numerical humility.” ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **ContextPipe: Database-Inspired Context Assembly for Long-Horizon Agents** — Peng Xu et al. frame agent context construction as query execution with planning, optimization, cache awareness, and EXPLAIN-style tracing. Worth tracking alongside Agentic Transaction, SemIso, and semantic-operator compilation. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **Modelpedia: A Catalog of Model Findings for the Meta-Science of AI** — Franciszek Bernat et al. propose an LLM-assisted catalog of claims about models, datasets, methods, and concepts extracted from papers. If the schema and curation quality hold up, this could become useful infrastructure for suppressing duplicate “model behavior” findings. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Can LLMs Use Relational Transformer Embeddings?** — Francisco Galuppo Azevedo and Clarissa Lima Loures report negative results for injecting frozen relational-encoder embeddings into Qwen3.5-4B as soft tokens over RelBench tasks. Paired with the context-window failure paper, it suggests relational/text fusion needs deeper alignment than projection-plus-LoRA. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))
