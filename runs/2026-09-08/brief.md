## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Xiaomi-TabLDM: A Tabular Foundation Model Technical Report**  
   **Authors:** Xiaomi-TabLDM Team: Penghui Wang, Wei Liu, Hong Wang, Chengyue Huang, Yuxi Sun, Zirui Wang, Hongming Huang, Quan Wang, Zhenwei Xin, Ping Hou, Jie Yu, Chunxiao Liu, Erli Meng, Bin Wang  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv, technical report  
   **Date:** submitted September 3, 2026; revised September 4, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03880))  
   **Summary:** This is the most relevant new tabular-foundation-model artifact I found that was not already in the delivered memory. Xiaomi-TabLDM is a synthetic-SCM-pretrained in-context model for tabular classification and regression, using a three-stage training recipe, dual-stream feature grouping, attention residuals, sparse MoE components, and test-time compute scaling. The claims are unusually ambitious: first on OpenML-CTR23, second across several regression suites, and substantially lower training/prediction time than TabFM on TabArena regression. The paper matters less as a fully settled comparison and more as evidence that tabular FM competition is shifting toward capacity scaling, MoE specialization, and inference-time scaling—not just better priors.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a serious signal that tabular FMs are entering a “systems plus scaling law” phase, with architecture/runtime choices becoming as important as the synthetic prior. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03880))

2. **A Theoretical Framework for Masked Pretraining (MPT)**  
   **Authors:** Qi Zhang, Runyu Zhou, Yifei Wang, Yisen Wang  
   **Venue/source:** JMLR Volume 27, Article 199  
   **Date:** 2026; newly visible in the current JMLR Volume 27 tail  
   **Link:** ([jmlr.org](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v27/25-0477.html))  
   **Summary:** This JMLR paper gives masked reconstruction pretraining a representation-learning theory parallel to contrastive learning. The key move is to show that masking implicitly constructs semantically related positive pairs and that reconstruction pulls their representations together. That helps explain why masked pretraining can extract useful invariant structure, but also exposes a dimensional-collapse failure mode. The authors propose Uniformity-enhanced MPT, prove downstream guarantees, and analyze how masking strategy affects representation quality. This is worth elevating because masked objectives are now central not only in vision/language but also in tabular, time-series, graph, and multimodal structured-data pretraining.  
   **Why you should care:** It gives a reusable theoretical vocabulary for comparing reconstruction, contrastive, and hybrid self-supervised objectives across structured-data foundation models. ([jmlr.org](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v27/25-0477.html))

3. **Persistent Teacher Anchoring for Tool-Using Agents**  
   **Authors:** Hyun Bin Park, Kyungho Song, Sangmin Lee, Du-Seong Chang  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv; accepted at EMNLP 2026 main conference  
   **Date:** submitted September 4, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04773))  
   **Summary:** The paper targets a subtle but important failure in on-policy distillation for tool-using agents: once a student generates tool calls that the teacher would not have made, environment observations feed back into later prefixes and the teacher-student distribution gap compounds. Persistent Teacher Anchoring lets the student propose trajectories but requires teacher commitment before a whole tool-use turn reaches the environment. It also adds persistent lookahead to use rollout capacity more efficiently. The reported gains—roughly 2.5–2.8 macro best@4 points over OPKD in retrieval/perception RL settings and 24% throughput improvement—make this a credible training-protocol contribution.  
   **Why you should care:** It treats tool execution as part of the supervised object, not a side effect of text generation, which is exactly the right abstraction for reliable data-analysis agents. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04773))

4. **Beyond Stationarity in Time Series: Discovering Causal Structures and Latent Regimes via Markov Blankets**  
   **Authors:** Lei Zan, Charles K. Assaad, Emilie Devijver, Eric Gaussier  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv; accepted at the 11th AALTD Workshop at ECML PKDD 2026  
   **Date:** submitted September 4, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05150))  
   **Summary:** RCBNB-MB attacks causal discovery in nonstationary time series by jointly segmenting latent regimes and learning regime-specific causal graphs. Rather than assume one stable temporal DAG, it identifies subsets of time points within which a causal structure is stable, using Markov blankets to preserve predictive information and reduce sensitivity to parent-discovery errors. The authors claim theoretical recovery guarantees for regime transitions and causal graphs, and evaluate on simulated data plus real IT-monitoring data. This is timely because many causal/time-series benchmarks still bake in stationarity assumptions that fail in operational telemetry, clinical trajectories, markets, and infrastructure systems.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a plausible bridge between causal discovery, regime detection, and production monitoring—three areas that structured-data ML increasingly needs to unify. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05150))

5. **On the Abundance of Critical Points of the t-SNE Energy**  
   **Authors:** Nakul Haridas, Ryan Murray  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Date:** submitted September 3, 2026  
   **Link:** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04379))  
   **Summary:** This is a mathematical paper on why t-SNE’s nonconvex energy landscape can produce embeddings with topology breaks and spurious clusters. For a family of energies including original t-SNE and large-data-limit variants, the authors construct infinite families of critical points when the input density has continuous symmetries. The construction pairs discrete symmetries in feature space with discrete symmetries in embedding space that are preserved under gradient dynamics. This is not directly about foundation models, but it speaks to a recurring issue in representation evaluation: low-dimensional pictures are optimization artifacts as much as geometry reports.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a useful warning shot for interpretability and representation-analysis workflows that still lean on t-SNE/UMAP-style visual evidence. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04379))

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Tabular FMs are becoming an engineering race, not just a prior-design race.** Xiaomi-TabLDM combines synthetic SCM pretraining with MoE, attention residuals, multi-stage training, and test-time compute scaling, while recent delivered items focused on compression, context selection, distillation, relational extensions, and failure modes. The center of gravity is moving from “does PFN-style pretraining work?” to “what is the scalable architecture/runtime envelope?”

- **Representation objectives are being audited for hidden degeneracies.** The MPT theory paper, the JEPA physical-laziness paper, the Graph-JEPA collapse work from recent briefings, and t-SNE critical-point analysis all point to the same meta-lesson: good aggregate losses or healthy-looking embeddings can hide semantically useless representations.

- **Causal ML is drifting toward operational nonstationarity.** Recent papers on regime-aware causal discovery, hierarchical/longitudinal causal models, stochastic interventions, transportability, and corporate-family/entity resolution all push beyond clean iid causal datasets toward changing environments, partial observability, and system-level data provenance.

- **Agent training protocols are absorbing database concerns.** Persistent Teacher Anchoring, semantic isolation/agentic transactions from prior briefs, and context/data-management work all treat agent behavior as stateful execution over mutable external systems, not just sequence modeling.

- **Evaluation validity remains a first-class research object.** Conformal failures under peer conformity, selective-risk control for extraction, active model evaluation, and JMLR’s new online multiple-testing/conformal-selection work are converging on one point: benchmark scores without deployment-specific sampling and dependence assumptions are increasingly suspect.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **REFINE: LLM Refinement over Budgeted Text-Attributed Graphs for Personalized Medical Concept Representation.** A patient-specific EHR representation method that builds temporal graphs from text-attributed medical KGs, uses an RL policy to choose expansion budgets, and refines graph embeddings with frozen-LLM soft prompts. Worth tracking as structured clinical representation learning moves from global code embeddings to patient-conditioned semantics. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04415))

- **ProToMEx: Rapid, Interpretable Explanations via Structured Representations.** A model-agnostic explainer that uses probabilistic topic models to give local/global explanations as latent “reasons” rather than atomic feature attributions, with reported 30–40× speedups over SHAP/LIME on tabular and synthetic datasets. Worth watching if it releases code or stronger fidelity/faithfulness audits. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04265))

- **OptunaHub: A Platform for Black-Box Optimization.** JMLR MLOSS paper introducing a decentralized Optuna-compatible registry for BBO algorithms and benchmark problems. It could become useful infrastructure for reproducible HPO, bandit-style production tuning, and experimental comparisons across AutoML communities. ([jmlr.org](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v27/25-2424.html))

- **Unveiling the Statistical Foundations of Chain-of-Thought Prompting Methods.** JMLR paper modeling CoT as approximate Bayesian estimation under a latent multi-step reasoning process, with prompting and pretraining error decomposition. Worth suppressing from future repeats because it may resurface in theory-of-reasoning discussions. ([jmlr.org](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v27/25-2240.html))
