## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Data Scarcity and Model Sparsity: Mixtures-of-Experts Overfit More to Repeated Data**  
   **Authors:** Atindra Jha, Margaret Li, Jure Leskovec, Percy Liang, Luke Zettlemoyer  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** September 11, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv listing. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   This is the most consequential item in today’s stream. It attacks a question that will matter more as “unique high-quality data” becomes a binding constraint: what happens when sparse MoE models are trained with repeated data? Across 80M–1B active-parameter models, up to 8.5B total parameters, MoEs degrade faster than dense transformers under repetition; the degradation worsens with sparsity and appears governed by total rather than active parameters. The paper also links the failure to early-stabilizing routing and expert specialization, while showing masking/dropout-like regularization only partially repairs the damage.  
   **Why you should care:** MoE compute efficiency may carry a hidden data-efficiency penalty, especially in repeated-data or synthetic-data-heavy regimes.

2. **The information geometry of large language models is shared, learned, and controllable**  
   **Authors:** Dario Picozzi  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** September 11, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv listing. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   This paper argues that next-token probability geometry, viewed through Fisher–Rao structure, is more stable and transferable than activation-space geometry. Across transformer, state-space, and recurrent models, the author reports stronger agreement in output geometries than in activation geometries, with shared geometry supporting semantic-category transfer and minimum-disturbance interventions. The most interesting claim is not another representation-similarity metric, but a proposal that behavioral geometry can guide steering, editing, attribution, dictionary learning, and fine-tuning with less collateral disruption than Euclidean controls. The claims are broad and need scrutiny, but the framing is elegant.  
   **Why you should care:** It reframes representation control around output-distribution geometry rather than coordinate-dependent hidden states.

3. **Learning Orthogonal Multi-Index Models Beyond Small Initialization: Incremental Learning, Competitive Dynamics and Symmetry**  
   **Authors:** Mo Zhou, Weihang Xu, Simon S. Du, Maryam Fazel  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** September 11, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv listing. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   A substantial 102-page theory paper on how two-layer networks learn orthogonal multi-index targets under standard initialization and polynomial samples. The key contribution is to move beyond simplifying assumptions—small initialization, correlation losses, layerwise training—that suppress neuron interactions. The authors prove incremental learning: lower-order Hermite components are learned before higher-order components recover target directions. They also describe competitive mass reallocation, where parameter mass first fits the mean, then moves into target subspaces, then concentrates on aligned neurons. The symmetry-based finite-width approximation may be useful beyond this model class.  
   **Why you should care:** It gives a sharper account of feature-learning dynamics in non-lazy regimes relevant to representation-learning theory.

4. **EGGROLL, Unrolled: Understanding and Improving Low-Rank Evolution Strategies at Scale**  
   **Authors:** Ege C. Kaya, Abolfazl Hashemi  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** September 11, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv listing. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   Low-rank evolution strategies are attractive for LLM post-training because dense perturbations are too expensive, but rank-one perturbations are geometrically severe. This paper analyzes EGGROLL’s finite-rank update field, showing it can introduce nonconservative components and even reverse local stability, while remaining exact on quadratics. The practical contribution, LOO-ROLL, replaces two antithetic evaluations per direction with one leave-one-out estimator, preserving the finite-rank population field and reducing estimator MSE. Reported matched-wall-time experiments up to 8B parameters show large GSM8K gains in several settings.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a rare combination of optimizer geometry, finite-rank perturbation theory, and plausible LLM-scale post-training relevance.

5. **Legible Failures: Detecting and Repairing In-Context Binding Errors**  
   **Authors:** Manas Venkata Sai Ravulapalli, Samrath Singh Chadha, Abhinav M. Hari  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Release date:** September 11, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv listing. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   The paper asks whether wrong answers reflect absent information or failure to use available information. On an entity–obligation binding task, a model can output the wrong prompt-supplied binding while a linear probe recovers the correct one from frozen hidden states. The authors evaluate 16 checkpoints, show probe-output disagreement improves failure detection over model confidence, and—most importantly—show residual-stream steering toward the probe-decoded binding improves accuracy without gold labels. This is a valuable counterpoint to work finding probe-detected errors hard to repair.  
   **Why you should care:** It identifies a class of representation failures where probes are not merely diagnostic but actionable.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Sparse models are becoming data-regime sensitive objects.** The MoE repetition paper, recent Muon/optimizer work, and TMLR’s continuing attention to routing, SSMs, and model-based RL suggest that architecture gains increasingly need to be evaluated jointly with data scarcity, repetition, and post-training protocols—not only FLOPs.

- **Representation evaluation is moving from similarity to actionability.** Today’s Fisher–Rao geometry paper and binding-error repair paper both ask whether a representation metric supports interventions with controlled side effects. That is a better bar than “is the feature linearly decodable?”

- **Certification is spreading into operational settings.** Conformal calibration transfer, crash-severity certification, model routing controls, SQL/data-system verification, and per-field/document-extraction risk control are converging on a common theme: finite-sample or protocol-bounded claims attached to deployed workflows.

- **Data systems for AI are becoming curation/provenance substrates.** OmniTable, REVA, prior BLIP/ContextPipe/Agentic Transaction work, and Management Science’s AI-review governance cluster all point toward persistent, auditable evidence layers as a central AI-systems problem.

- **Synthetic and domain-specific time-series resources keep proliferating.** LoaDiff, AmazonSWE, CGM forecasting evaluation, WaveletDiff, and high-frequency-network TMLR resources reinforce that “time-series foundation model” evaluation is fragmenting into domain-specific regimes where zero-shot claims often weaken.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **OmniTable: A Unified Wide-Table System for Petabyte-Scale LLM Data Curation and Exploration.** Full arXiv/PVLDB listing for the VLDB 2026 Best Industry Paper: a unified logical wide-table layer over 35PB of LLM training data, with declarative feature lifecycle management, lineage, adaptive execution, CPU/GPU routing, and reported curation-cycle reduction from about 14 days to 2.5 days. Track this as a serious industrial data-management blueprint for foundation-model corpora. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **AmazonSWE dataset.** A large, extremely sparse spatiotemporal graph imputation benchmark for Amazon river water-surface elevation: 19K+ river sections over 2016–2026, less than 1% observed per day, directed acyclic river topology, and held-out in situ gauges. Good benchmark design for structured missingness and graph/time foundation models. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **LoaDiff.** ICDM 2026 diffusion model for year-long, sub-hourly electricity-consumption time series, with static household and dynamic weather/calendar conditioning, evaluated for fidelity, memorization risk, downstream forecasting/appliance utility, and controllability. Worth suppressing because it is a concrete synthetic-data artifact for privacy-constrained energy analytics. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Black-Box Membership Inference via Word-Level Probability Estimation.** Strict black-box MIA using sampled continuations and local smoothing to estimate word-level probabilities, with reported evaluations on proprietary systems including GPT-5-Chat, Gemini-2.5-Flash, and Claude-4.5-Haiku. Treat the proprietary-model claims cautiously, but the access model is exactly where privacy audits are heading. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))

- **LTLBench.** Newly visible TMLR September benchmark for temporal reasoning in LLMs. The listing alone is enough to watch; temporal logic remains a clean way to stress long-horizon state tracking and agent planning. ([jmlr.org](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/papers/))

- **Conformal Calibration Transfer.** ICML 2026 paper on transporting labeled source calibration into a target space using paired unlabeled observations, then correcting residual mismatch with label-free diagnostics or reweighting. Particularly relevant for sensor changes, modality shifts, and low-label deployment. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))
