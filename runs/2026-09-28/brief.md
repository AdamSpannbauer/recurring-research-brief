## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Nonparametric In-Context Learning under Growing Geometric Complexity: Minimax Optimality and Local Geometry-Adaptivity of Transformers**  
   **Authors:** Jaehee Seo, Jisu Kim  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv; accepted at NeurIPS 2026  
   **Publication/release date:** September 28, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv / NeurIPS 2026 listing via arXiv citation. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))  
   **Summary:** This is the most theory-rich item in today’s crop. The paper studies in-context prediction when tasks live on mixtures of manifolds whose dimensions, smoothness, masses, and local geometry may vary with sample size. It proves a minimax lower bound, constructs an oracle tangent local-polynomial estimator with matching rate, and then connects that estimator to a two-stage softmax Transformer with geometric preconditioning and chartwise local solvers. The key claim is not just that Transformers can approximate nonparametric estimators, but that they can adapt locally to heterogeneous geometry at near-minimax rates under explicit separation and perturbation conditions.  
   **Why you should care:** It gives a serious statistical lens on when ICL should exploit latent geometry rather than just memorize examples.

2. **I Act Therefore I Am: When Is JEPA’s Action-Conditioning Enough to Learn Causal Mechanisms?**  
   **Authors:** Yuhang Liu, Zhuo Huang, Javen Qinfeng Shi  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Publication/release date:** September 28, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Summary:** The paper asks a foundational question for world models: when does action-conditioned prediction recover causal state rather than merely fit observations? It introduces a latent-variable model with high-dimensional observations generated from latent causal states whose transitions are action-conditioned, then proposes an information-theoretic objective combining conditional likelihood for dynamics with entropy preservation for latent information. The theory identifies sufficient action-induced transition variation as central for identifiability up to componentwise invertible transformations and permutation. The instantiated A-JEPA objective is validated in synthetic and visual settings, including transfer to unseen transition mechanisms.  
   **Why you should care:** It sharpens the line between predictive world models and causally useful representations.

3. **Causal Retention in Interactive Agents: Interface Factorization and Selective Adaptation**  
   **Authors:** Shengjun Zhang, Tingyi Liu, Dong Xie, Yunlong Dong, Xiang Wang, Cheng Zeng  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Publication/release date:** September 28, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Summary:** This paper formalizes “causal retention”: whether a frozen learned state preserves mechanism information for probes fixed independently of training. The theory treats optimal probe error as Bayes decision risk over finite SCM classes, showing when interface fibers preserve probe-answer information and when post-processing cannot recover it. The proposed Causal Core implementation uses evidence-gated writing, readout filtering, temporal credit, hidden-context setup, and local diagnostic updates. Experiments span finite causal systems, continuous simulators, TD-MPC2 world models, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; the strongest result is that task sufficiency and source-domain decodability can mask failures under changed causal mechanisms.  
   **Why you should care:** It is a useful abstraction for evaluating whether agents retain the *right* causal mechanisms, not just task performance.

4. **Benchmarking Attention for Tabular Foundation Models**  
   **Authors:** Maximilian Schambach, Clemens Biehl, Sam Thelin  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv  
   **Publication/release date:** September 28, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Summary:** A practical but important systems paper for tabular foundation models. It observes that TabPFN/Mitra/ConTextTab-style models use alternating row and column attention over 2D latent arrays, creating different sequence lengths, memory layouts, and head-dimension regimes than language-model attention. The authors benchmark Torch SDPA/cuDNN, FlashAttention 2/3/4, vLLM, and SageAttention across realistic tabular shapes and A100/H100/B200 hardware. The main finding is that optimal backend choice differs between row and column attention and is hardware/model-shape dependent; FlashAttention often wins, but cuDNN can beat it for some column-attention regimes.  
   **Why you should care:** TFM scaling is becoming bottlenecked by table-native systems details, not just architecture.

5. **Training Graph Foundation Models on The Web Graph**  
   **Authors:** Ryoma Sato  
   **Venue/source:** arXiv cross-list from cs.AI  
   **Publication/release date:** September 28, 2026  
   **Link:** arXiv. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
   **Summary:** This technical report introduces **Acacia**, a graph foundation model trained from scratch on the Common Crawl web graph. The claimed capabilities are unusually broad: arbitrary feature dimensionalities and semantics without extra training, node classification, link prediction, clustering, graph generation, and in-context learning, without stitching to a pretrained LLM. The paper is ambitious and should be read skeptically until independent evaluation appears, but the direction is important: treating web-scale graph structure itself as a pretraining substrate for graph/relational reasoning, rather than converting everything to text or task-specific GNN heads.  
   **Why you should care:** If the claims hold, graph FMs may start to look more like native foundation models than LLM add-ons.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Structured-data foundation models are moving from accuracy to infrastructure.** The attention-backend benchmark for TFMs, graph FM pretraining on web-scale graphs, and demand/time-series model specialization all point to a new phase: deployment bottlenecks, memory layout, data modality, and corpus construction now matter as much as model class.
- **Causal representation learning is converging with world-model research.** JEPA identifiability, causal retention in agents, design-respecting epidemiological world models, and non-Gaussian causal discovery all ask whether a learned state preserves the intervention-relevant mechanism, not merely predictive signal.
- **Conformal and uncertainty methods are becoming feedback-aware.** Counterfactual online conformal prediction, missingness-aware hospital calibration, and exponential-tilt joint shift papers all address failures caused by deployment loops, target shift, and heterogeneous missingness rather than IID calibration.
- **Interpretability evaluation is becoming more measurement-theoretic.** The quantization noise-floor paper, exact MLP soft-gate decomposition, cross-layer concept discovery, and causal-retention probes all emphasize whether an interpretability statistic is actually identifiable, stable, or actionable.
- **Database research is absorbing AI-agent concerns but still producing hard theory.** Sep. 28 cs.DB spans vector-index construction walls, HAVING-query provenance with Lean formalization, bag-containment decidability, and dataspace/agent governance.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **Counterfactual Online Conformal Prediction Under Adaptive Logging** — Xinyu Qiao, Yichen Lin, Kaihong Ji, Xue Wang, Tao Yao. Introduces propensity-weighted and doubly robust online conformal calibration for settings where predictions affect actions and actions determine which outcomes enter calibration. This is directly relevant to bandit-driven data collection and adaptive product/agent systems. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))
- **Provenance of HAVING Queries in Semirings with Monus** — Aryak Sen, Pratik Karmakar, Silviu Maniu, Angelo Saadeh, Pierre Senellart. Extends semiring provenance to aggregate/HAVING queries using m-semirings, with ProvSQL implementation and Lean formalization. This is a rare combination of database theory, provenance, probabilistic query evaluation, and mechanized semantics. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))
- **EXAONE Demand 1.0: A Time Series Foundation Model for Demand Forecasting** — Seunghan Lee et al. A demand-specialized TSFM using 11.3M series / 48.4B observations, synthetic demand generation, class-specific LoRA-style branches, and a statistics-driven router over smooth/intermittent/erratic/lumpy demand. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))
- **MARCEDES: Score-based causal discovery under non-Gaussianity with continuous optimization** — Anamitra Chaudhuri, Anirban Bhattacharya, Yang Ni. Uses mean absolute residual risk motivated by Laplace SEM misspecification, row-specific sparsity penalties, and generalized-Bayes tuning for continuous DAG learning. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))
- **Batched Feedback and the Random-Access Wall in Search-Based Graph Construction** — Édgar Chávez. A careful systems study of graph construction for nearest-neighbor search, showing batched feedback recovers some incremental benefits but adaptive random access remains a hard wall relative to dense blocked kernels. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))
