## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **When Does Synthetic Relational Data Teach Models to Use Relations? Tracing Predictive Structure from Pretraining Data to Model Behavior** — Shivam Dubey, Mohamed Bouadi, Nassim Bouarour, Varun Kulkarni, Aditya Tanna, Vinay Kumar Sankarapu. **arXiv cs.LG**, listed **2026-10-05**. Link: arXiv listing.  
This is the most directly relevant item this cycle: it asks what property of synthetic relational pretraining data actually causes a relational foundation model to *use* cross-table structure, rather than merely learn good row-level statistics. The authors compare four Relational Transformer checkpoints trained under matched architecture, initialization, objective, and compute, but on corpora from different relational-data generators. Their central diagnostic is whether foreign-key-linked parents are predictively necessary for masked-cell pretraining. RelDiff produces the strongest cross-table predictive gain; its trained model is uniquely sensitive to foreign-key interventions, and disrupting the mechanism at inference removes its downstream advantage. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
**Why you should care:** This is a rare bridge from synthetic relational-data design to learned relational computation, not just benchmark accuracy.

2. **From Behavior to Provenance: Attributing Tabular Foundation Models to Synthetic Pretraining Data** — Mohamed Bouadi, Nassim Bouarour, Shivam Dubey, Aditya Tanna, Vinay Kumar Sankarapu. **arXiv cs.LG**, listed **2026-10-05**. Link: arXiv listing.  
This companion-style paper tackles a foundational problem for tabular FMs: how to validate claims that particular synthetic pretraining tasks shaped downstream behavior. Using **O’PRIOR**, a provenance-rich synthetic-task generator, every pretraining task carries lineage over mechanisms, missingness, confounding, shortcuts, and distribution shift. The paper combines behavior-conditioned attribution, counterfactual retraining, and provenance-aware interventions. Removing top-attributed synthetic tasks reduces mean ROC-AUC much more than random removal; targeted removal of shortcut-provenance tasks has an even larger effect. The nuance is important: provenance association is only modest by ranking AUROC, showing that attribution and interventional faithfulness can diverge. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
**Why you should care:** Synthetic-prior tabular FMs need provenance audits; this gives a controlled experimental language for doing them.

3. **Drive vs. Decay: On the Training Dynamics of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures** — José Lucas De Melo Costa, Seong Woo Ahn, Fabrice Popineau, Arpad Rimmel, Bich-Liên Doan. **arXiv / NeurIPS 2026 main track**, listed **2026-10-05**. Link: arXiv listing.  
JEPA-style self-supervised models are attractive for representation learning but fragile to collapse. This paper develops an early-training stability theory by linearizing coupled JEPA gradient flow around the trivial fixed point, yielding a per-mode stability ratio between “drive” and “decay.” The theory predicts which representation modes become unstable and therefore emerge, and it unifies predictor scaling, masking ratio, and EMA as mechanisms that shift the stability boundary. The empirical sweep is unusually relevant: more than 800 **Tabular-JEPA** configurations, plus image-domain I-JEPA experiments. The proposed ResidualPred predictor initializes attention near identity and improves effective rank and downstream accuracy. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
**Why you should care:** It turns JEPA collapse avoidance from recipes into a mode-wise dynamical criterion, with explicit tabular experiments.

4. **Causal Representation Learning with Instantaneous and Lagged Relations via Nonstationarity** — Tatsuya Yamada, Hiroshi Morioka, Yoshinobu Kawahara. **arXiv cs.LG**, listed **2026-10-05**. Link: arXiv listing.  
This paper addresses a subtle but important setting: latent time-series causal representation learning when both lagged relations and within-interval instantaneous relations matter, and when nonstationarity provides identifying variation. The authors prove sufficient conditions for identifying latent states up to permutation and component-wise invertible transformations, as well as their instantaneous and lagged causal structures, using an observed auxiliary variable such as time or condition label associated with changes in transition-noise distributions. The proposed iCReN framework uses contrastive learning with discrete or continuous auxiliary variables and is evaluated on synthetic recovery tasks plus downstream forecasting. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))  
**Why you should care:** This is close to the causal-representation sweet spot: exploiting naturally occurring nonstationarity as weak supervision for latent causal structure.

5. **ArrivalBench: Agent-Generated Data Pipelines Are Correct Once and Wrong Under Time** — Pranay Kothari. **arXiv cs.LG/cs.DB; NeurIPS 2026 Workshop “Who Verifies the Agents?”**, listed **2026-10-05**. Link: arXiv listing.  
ArrivalBench is a sharp benchmark idea for data-agent reliability. Instead of grading an agent-generated pipeline by running it once on a fixed snapshot, it re-executes the artifact under replayable delivery schedules with late, duplicated, out-of-order, and retried records. The oracle is batch recomputation of the complete log, so silent wrong tables are distinguished from crashes. On 40 tasks, a single-execution grader certifies 86–100% of pipelines from eleven models, but replay finds 7.0–79.2% of those certified artifacts silently wrong. Idempotency hazards are worse than ordering hazards, and simple warnings can trade silent failure for visible crash without solving correctness. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))  
**Why you should care:** This is exactly the kind of temporal semantics benchmark data agents need before they can be trusted with operational pipelines.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Synthetic data is becoming diagnosable, not just useful.** The new relational-pretraining and TFM-provenance papers move from “synthetic priors help” to “which synthetic mechanisms produce which learned computations?” That is a significant maturation for tabular/relational FMs.

- **Data-agent evaluation is shifting from snapshot correctness to temporal semantics.** ArrivalBench, state-footprint transactions, feature freshness budgets, and Zephon all point to the same failure mode: an artifact can be correct on one static run and fail under time, retries, topology changes, or stream lag.

- **Self-supervised representation learning is getting more dynamical.** JEPA collapse, causal latent time-series identification, and self-supervised feature acquisition all use training or test-time dynamics—not just final embeddings—as the object of analysis.

- **Vector/search systems are absorbing statistical guarantees.** RaBitQ-SSD and SOLO are part of a growing push to pair approximate retrieval with recall, distance, or pruning certificates rather than heuristic ANN performance alone.

- **TMLR’s October stream is heavy on infrastructure-level ML reliability.** Calibration, encoder stealing, OOD detection, transport, KG reasoning, and retrieval LoRA suggest a venue tilt toward evaluability and deployment behavior rather than just architectures.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **Counterfactual Predictions in Scientific Emulators Without Controlled Experiments / ReRoute** — Dingling Yao, Kahaan Gandhi, Valentin Duruisseaux, Boris Bonev, Francesco Locatello, Anima Anandkumar. ReRoute fine-tunes scientific emulators for counterfactual “what-if” prediction using factual data plus partial mechanistic knowledge, with a Lean-checked identification argument and climate-emulation experiments. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Measure Less, Know More: Self-Supervised Test-Time Feature Acquisition / ECHO-k** — Eeshaan Jain, Linus Bleistein, Bart Deplancke, Charlotte Bunne. A NeurIPS 2026 paper using pretrained representations as self-supervised proxy targets for task-agnostic sequential modality acquisition. Useful for costly-feature tabular/multimodal deployment. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new))

- **Zephon: Elastic Determinism for Online, Stateful Foundation Model Data Loading Pipelines** — Maximilian Böther et al. A foundation-model data-loader design for deterministic global batches under topology changes, checkpoint-resume, and online stateful tokenization/packing/mixing. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **RaBitQ-SSD: Split Codes and Pipelined I/O for SSD-Resident Vector Search** — Yuexuan Xu et al. Extends IVF-RaBitQ to SSD-resident vector search with split binary codes, probabilistic error bounds, candidate pruning, and pipelined I/O up to 10B vectors on one SSD. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **COSTAR: Improved Temporal Counterfactual Estimation with Self-Supervised Learning** — Chuizheng Meng, Yihe Dong, Sercan O Arik, Yan Liu, Tomas Pfister. Newly visible TMLR October paper; worth tracking as a causal/time-series SSL bridge. ([jmlr.org](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/papers/))
