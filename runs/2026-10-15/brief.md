## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Semi-Amortized Bayesian Inference with Reusable Local Posteriors** — Niek Stevenson, Paul-Christian Bürkner, Andrew Heathcote, Stefan T. Radev. **arXiv stat.ML**, submitted **2026-10-13**. Link: ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18945))  
   This is the most generally useful idea in today’s batch for probabilistic ML: rather than amortizing a full high-dimensional posterior, the paper decomposes inference into reusable local conditional-posterior estimators. A Markov chain alternates neural conditional draws for expensive/repeated blocks with conventional updates for the rest, avoiding likelihood evaluation and accept/reject correction for those learned updates. The key conceptual move is to make amortization modular: the output dimension is set by the block, not by the full model, and repeated conditional structure can be reused across blocks or datasets. The examples target hierarchical and spatiotemporal models.  
   **Why you should care:** this is a plausible route to “posterior components as reusable learned operators,” which fits directly into simulation-based Bayes, probabilistic programming, and scalable Bayesian workflow design.

2. **Discrete Diffusion Models are Minimax Optimal Under Sparsity** — Luca Blunda, Tyler Farghly, Umut Simsekli, Alain Durmus. **arXiv stat.ML**, submitted **2026-10-13**. Link: ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19020))  
   The paper gives a clean statistical-theory result for discrete diffusion models, focusing on categorical/text-like data where the theoretical picture is still much thinner than for continuous diffusion. It studies a nonparametric estimator derived from a regularized score-entropy objective and proves minimax-optimal rates in sparse regimes. The regularization resembles label smoothing and, importantly, does not require knowing the sparsity parameter. The broader message is that discrete diffusion can intrinsically adapt to sparse structure, not merely compete empirically with autoregressive models.  
   **Why you should care:** this strengthens the case that diffusion-style language models have real statistical structure-adaptivity, not just engineering appeal.

3. **Generalizing Identifiability Results in Causal Discovery from Complete Data to Missing Data Settings** — Matteo Ceriscioli, Shohei Shimizu, Mallika Gupta, Karthika Mohan. **arXiv stat.ML**, submitted **2026-10-13**. Link: ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19211))  
   This paper attacks a persistent gap in causal discovery: most identifiability results assume complete data, while real structured datasets often have informative missingness. It establishes sufficient conditions under which causal graphs remain identifiable from available-data distributions across missingness categories, including MNAR. The main preservation conditions rule out causal interaction among missingness mechanisms, self-caused missingness, and require either faithfulness for missingness mechanisms or generalized-linear missingness models. The authors then give two asymptotically correct algorithms for additive noise models that recover the graph and consistent parameters.  
   **Why you should care:** it gives a bridge from elegant complete-data causal discovery theory to the messy missing-data regimes common in healthcare, enterprise, and survey data.

4. **State-Matched Sequence Conformal Prediction** — Ruihang Han, Li-Hsiang Lin. **arXiv stat.ML**, submitted **2026-10-14**. Link: ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19484))  
   Sequential conformal prediction typically calibrates against recent errors or online residual feedback, but those can fail when comparable states recur nonlocally in time. SMSCP constructs an explicit one-sided local-linear state from pre-target information, retrieves state-similar historical calibration scores, and applies the ordinary unweighted conformal order statistic to that matched reference set. The paper analyzes when nearby states have comparable target-score quantiles under temporal dependence and empirically reports better interval scores on solar irradiance and bike-demand benchmarks, with closer nominal coverage than ResCP on five of six benchmarks.  
   **Why you should care:** it reframes sequential conformal calibration as representation/retrieval over latent states, a useful idea for time-series foundation models and adaptive forecasting systems.

5. **Weighted Adjoint Matching: Finetuning Flow Matching Models with Time-Dependent Control Cost** — Eliot Beyler, Francis Bach. **arXiv stat.ML**, submitted **2026-10-14**. Link: ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20035))  
   This paper extends Adjoint Matching for reward fine-tuning pretrained diffusion/flow-matching models by making the quadratic control cost time-dependent. The intuition is compelling: early generation controls coarse semantic structure, so interventions there should be expensive to preserve diversity and balance; late controls affect details, so they can be cheaper for reward improvement. The authors prove equivalence to reward maximization under time-varying KL regularization over generative paths and show better reward/mode-balance tradeoffs than standard Adjoint Matching on Gaussian mixtures and MNIST.  
   **Why you should care:** it is a principled recipe for post-training generative models without collapsing early semantic diversity, relevant to reward steering, synthetic data, and diffusion/flow RL.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **Reliability evaluation is moving from point metrics to calibrated operational guarantees.** Today’s conformal papers and semantic-query certification work continue a broader shift toward finite-sample guarantees, matched calibration sets, and deployment-aware risk budgets rather than aggregate accuracy.

- **Diffusion/flow theory is becoming more granular.** Recent work is no longer just “diffusion works”; it asks when discrete diffusion adapts to sparsity, how fine-tuning should allocate pathwise KL over time, and which inverse-problem noise models need non-Gaussian noising processes.

- **Bayesian amortization is becoming modular.** Reusable local posteriors, simulator-based empirical Bayes, mixed neural posterior estimation, and conditional-block inference all point toward learned inference components that can be plugged into larger probabilistic workflows.

- **Causal ML is broadening toward imperfect data regimes.** Missingness, semicontinuous outcomes, unstructured surrogates, causal discovery under selection bias, and data-release coarsening are now central, not edge cases.

- **Venue activity around agents is shifting from demos to contracts.** TMLR and arXiv continue to surface agent benchmarks and enterprise-agent papers, but the more interesting work is about traceability, verification, evidence contracts, and calibrated decision thresholds.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **Convergence Rates of Gaussian Process Thompson Sampling for Efficient Global Optimization** — Haowei Wang, Yilin Zheng. Proves Matérn-kernel GP-TS simple-regret rates and introduces GP-CTS, which caps posterior sample paths with a valid RKHS norm bound to remove an intrinsic logarithmic penalty. Worth suppressing because it may matter for Bayesian optimization theory. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19925))

- **Bayesian Causal Forests for Zero-Inflated Continuous Outcomes** — Hugo Gobato Souto, Eric Wang, Igor Shuryak. ZIC-BCF-Smear is a two-part nonparametric HTE estimator for semicontinuous outcomes with an exact zero mass, using Probit BCF for participation, Gaussian BCF for positive intensity, subpopulation propensity adjustment, and Duan smearing. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18889))

- **SNACK: A Sequential Notation Framework for Probabilistic Graph Generation** — Hohyun Kim, Hyesung Kim, Min-hwan Oh, Seunggeun Lee. A NeurIPS 2026 paper that linearizes graph generation into valid sequences, supports invalid-logit masking for 100% valid generated sequences, and enables string-based GFlowNet training with node-ordering symmetries. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/stat.ML/new))

- **Automated Standardization of Legacy Biomedical Metadata Using an Ontology-Constrained LLM Agent / ARMS** — Josef Hardi et al. A tool-augmented LLM agent dynamically retrieves machine-actionable metadata templates and biomedical terminology; on 839 HuBMAP legacy records it improves precision and recall over prompt-only standardization. Useful for data-integration and FAIR metadata workflows. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))

- **TEAR: Table Extraction with Attribute Recommendation from Texts via Large Language Models** — Tong Li et al. Already noted earlier as a benchmark direction, but today’s cs.DB replacement reinforces the SIGMOD 2027 trajectory: open-ended attribute recommendation plus table extraction from naturally occurring text, not just table descriptions. Suppress further repeats unless the public benchmark materially expands. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.DB/new))
