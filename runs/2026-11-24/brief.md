## Section 1: Top 5 Papers

1. **Tabular Foundation Models for Weighted Regression and Classification** — **Junwen Xia, Yan He, Dehan Kong, Zhenhua Lin**. **arXiv**, submitted **November 22, 2026**. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29789))  
This is the most directly relevant new paper: it extends TabPFN-3.5-style in-context tabular foundation models to **sample-weighted prediction**, a missing capability for causal inference, missing-data analysis, survey/insurance weighting, and covariate-shift correction. The authors introduce weighted attention, a normalized weighted loss, and synthetic weighted pretraining tasks derived from missing-data settings, yielding **WeightedPFN**. The paper is especially interesting because it couples a practical TFM adaptation with theory: suitably pretrained weighted Transformers can attain nonparametric minimax rates for new weighted-regression tasks without task-specific fitting.  
**Why you should care:** weighted TFMs are a natural bridge between tabular foundation models and semiparametric/causal workflows.

2. **SignalCount: Approximate Counts with Structured and Semantic Predicates in Seconds** — **Matteo Agius-D’Arrigo, Zachary Zelner, Harrison Ju, Rushab Munot**. **arXiv**, submitted **November 23, 2026**. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30779))  
SignalCount targets a very concrete emerging DB/LLM workload: fast approximate `COUNT` queries where part of the predicate is structured and part requires semantic judgment over text. Instead of calling an LLM oracle at query time, it uses logged production LLM verdicts to calibrate a fast classifier offline; at query time it scans, samples up to 1,000 records, scores with the classifier, and returns a calibrated interval. The reported multi-tenant deployment has p99 latency of 3.8 seconds and roughly nominal interval coverage, though with aggregate upward bias.  
**Why you should care:** this is a serious production-style answer to semantic-query latency and uncertainty, not just another LLM-over-tables demo.

3. **Fast and Consistent Structure Learning in Graphical Models via Approximate Cross-Validation** — **Zhao Lyu, Mladen Kolar, Bryon Aragam**. **NeurIPS 2026 / arXiv**, submitted **November 23, 2026**. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30822))  
This paper attacks a long-standing tension in sparse graphical-model structure learning: cross-validation is flexible but computationally expensive and traditionally inconsistent for structure recovery, while information criteria require model-specific calibration. The authors propose an approximate-cross-validation procedure that is both scalable and selection-consistent under signal-strength assumptions. For Adam’s interests, the broader idea is the methodological pattern: take a robust practical model-selection tool and repair its structural-inference defects without retreating to heavily hand-calibrated likelihood criteria.  
**Why you should care:** it may become a useful template for graph/causal discovery model selection under modern flexible estimators.

4. **Beyond Patching: Consistent Circuit Discovery via Gradient Spectral Analysis** — **Yujie Zhang, Tianyi Zhao, Guanghui Min, Chen Chen**. **arXiv**, submitted **November 23, 2026**. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30939))  
This interpretability paper proposes **Spectral Circuit Analysis**, replacing prompt-pair activation patching with a gradient-spectral view of task structure. The core claim is that causally relevant directions are recoverable from the spectral structure of forward gradients across paraphrases: leading directions concentrate task signal, while trailing directions absorb paraphrase noise. A block-wise SVD produces task subspaces and per-unit rankings, competitive with attribution-patching baselines across twelve tasks in GPT-2 Small and Pythia-160M, while also exposing cross-task shared units.  
**Why you should care:** it directly addresses a current fragility in circuit discovery: dependence on hand-crafted clean/corrupt prompt pairs.

5. **Mind the Trap As LLMs Guess the Data Model: Grain-Aware Guards Against Fan-out in Text-to-SQL and Its Benchmarks** — **Ahmed M. Mahran**. **arXiv**, submitted **November 21, 2026**. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29009))  
This is a clean database-systems warning shot for LLM Text-to-SQL. The paper shows that many plausible aggregate queries silently overcount when one-to-many or many-to-many joins fan out the measure’s grain. The author audits BIRD and finds distorted gold answers in 4.2% of dev questions, releases **BIRD-FanOut** and **GrainTraps**, and shows that grain-aware functional dependencies reduce fan-out errors across eight LLM agents. The lesson is sharp: schemas and ordinary FDs are insufficient; systems need measure-grain metadata and benchmark gold needs data-level auditing.  
**Why you should care:** it identifies a benchmark and production failure mode that looks like reasoning failure but is really missing data semantics.

## Section 3: Emerging Trends

- **TFMs are moving from “accuracy” to “statistical interfaces.”** The weighted TFM paper, TabReliBench-style reliability work from earlier briefings, and TabCUQ-like uncertainty papers indicate the next phase: TFMs must accept weights, return calibrated uncertainty, support missingness/causal designs, and behave under shift.

- **Semantic query processing is becoming statistical database engineering.** SignalCount, Semantic Views/SemReuse/OCTOPUS-style work, and today’s grain-aware Text-to-SQL paper all treat LLM predicates as noisy measurements requiring calibration, reuse contracts, provenance, and semantic metadata.

- **Interpretability is pivoting from local interventions to subspaces and invariances.** Gradient spectral circuit discovery fits a broader move away from brittle prompt-pair patching toward task-stable directions, spectral decompositions, and representation-level consistency.

- **Benchmarks are increasingly audited for hidden data-model assumptions.** BIRD-FanOut joins a growing set of “benchmark validity” papers showing that many evaluation errors arise from grain, difficulty measure, stochastic operators, or deployment mismatch rather than model competence alone.

- **Causal/graphical structure learning is reconnecting with scalable validation.** Approximate CV for graphical-model selection and false-positive-controlled causal discovery point toward structure learning methods that expose finite-sample error budgets rather than only asymptotic identifiability.

## Section 4: Worth Watching

- **RoFE: Rotated Frozen Embeddings for Low-Data Multimodal Tabular Learning** — a training-free fix for passing high-dimensional image/text embeddings into PFN-style tabular models; the key observation is that PCA plus per-column standardization can collapse labeled examples into a simplex, and random rotation restores geometry. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31049))

- **Bound to be Complete: Efficient Verification for SQL Equivalence via U-Expressions / SQLution** — a complete SQL-equivalence verifier using U-expressions, order-semantics encoding, and bounded summation expansion; relevant to Text-to-SQL verification and query-rewrite correctness. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28516))

- **BiPert: a unified linear framework for single-cell perturbation modeling** — a strong reminder that simple linear decompositions can beat deep perturbation models on forward prediction and inverse perturbation identification in unseen cell contexts. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31227))

- **Prediction-Powered Regression via Calibration** — calibrates pseudo-labels from abundant unlabeled/predicted data, gives nonasymptotic and minimax results, and is adjacent to prediction-powered inference and AI-assisted statistical estimation. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30976))

- **TrialAgentBench** — a NeurIPS 2026 E&D benchmark for clinical-trial analysis and long-horizon drug-development decisions; likely worth tracking when the artifact is easier to inspect. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent))
